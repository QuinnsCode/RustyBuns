# Two Fronts: Thunderwooka Saga

> **What it proves:** a turn-based strategy engine and three.js battles, on the ground and in orbit, built apart and joined by small JSON types. Against the AI or hotseat it runs in the page; online, each campaign is a Durable Object that keeps the turns in its SQLite. One of the [Rusty Buns](../../README.md#see-it-work) examples.

*The Wooka came with a furry ferocity unseen by the likes of man.*

The Thunder Marines landed on Doren, the forest moon, to clear out a few knee-high locals. They brought walkers, gunships and orbital strikes. The locals brought clubs, and they know every fern. Somewhere between the drop and the generator, the hunters became the hunted.

The Wooka have pushed into the galaxy, and only the Thunder Marines stand in their way. The Thunder Marines are the good guys here: black armour with white trim, full-face helmets with snorkel masks on the front, and backpacks far too big for anyone, bristling with antennas. The Wooka are round and fluffy, about knee-high, with big black eyes that have nothing behind them and clubs built to maim. The radio keeps reminding you that they are not cuddly.

*Hold the line* drops you straight into the default fight: a Thunder Marine holding the shield generator against the horde in a redwood forest. *Be the horde* plays it from the other side, as a Wooka chieftain. Add `?world=canyon` (desert), `capital` (ruined city), `cannon` (beach), `spaceport` (station) or `jungle` to the address to play either on another map. 🚀 holds an orbit against the Wooka fleet, picking orders round by round or flying a fighter. The campaign wraps those fights in a galactic war you have to win on two fronts. Fleets fight for the orbit of each world and armies fight for its sectors. Once a turn, you pick one battle and play it yourself as a hero: a ground battle, in a third-person fight over command posts, or a space battle for the orbit, fought by picking orders (or flown in a fighter). This builds on the plan in [notes/game-ideas/2-two-fronts.md](../../notes/game-ideas/2-two-fronts.md).

- **Who plays:** ↺ starts a campaign on any of the three maps, three ways. *Against the AI*: you're the Thunder Marines, and the Wooka AI moves when you end your turn. *Hotseat*: two players at one screen, handing it over between turns, so neither sees the other's orders. *Online*: you get a link to send whoever plays the Wooka, and you each hand in orders from your own link whenever you like. The turn resolves once both are in, and the page checks back every few seconds while it waits. Each side has its own hero, and a turn still has one hero battle: if both pick one, the Thunder Marines' pick goes first.
- **The war table:** the galaxy is a star map over nebulae. Each system is a glowing star with a name plaque and a dot per sector in its holder's colour. Its territory bulges round it, and a side's territories run together into one translucent sweep with a glowing border where it meets the enemy, hatched red when the enemy holds the orbit. Regions are dashed outlines, with a badge for their bonus that lights up for the side holding every capital in it. A bar along the top shows your credits and income, the war score, your armies, ships, regions and hero. The overview down the left lists every world you hold, with its sectors, armies and ships (click one to select it), and the next-turn button sits in the bottom corner. Armies are numbered counters and fleets numbered chevrons, a lane between two sides burns as the front line, and your orders are arrows with their count on the bend. The fog of war hides the enemy's numbers: you see who holds every world, but how many armies and ships they have there only where you have someone of your own. Elsewhere their pieces show a question mark. The panel's income line opens to break next turn's income down: sectors held, each region bonus, and the morale cut if you're losing. The panel says things with lucide icons and tooltips rather than words: send rows are an icon, a count, an arrow, a destination and a send button.
- **The galaxy:** the Thunder Marines against the Wooka, on a map you pick when you start a new campaign. *First contact* is the intro: ours, theirs and an empty place between with no ground to hold, where you learn capital-ship fighting before you land. The short war is eight systems in three regions, an evening's play. The full campaign is thirty systems in seven regions, over several evenings: each side's two home regions, with the High Drift, the Narrows and the Ember Belt between them. Hold every capital in a region for its bonus. Most worlds are one capital. On the short map, Kessa and Lumen, the two worlds of the Narrows, each have three sectors: a shield generator, a spaceport and a capital. Corva and Morrow, each side's way into its home region, each have an anti-ship cannon beside their capital. The full map has eight multi-sector worlds: a shipyard homeworld for each side, a shipyard in each outer middle region, shield worlds either side of the Narrows, and a cannon world guarding each way into a home region. Tarn and Umber, in the Narrows, each have a dust sea whose ore pays as well as a capital, and Rook and Xan each have a jungle, which its holder defends as if they held the orbit. *The Horde* is sixty worlds in twelve regions, twelve columns of five, the Thunder Marines' half to the west and the Wooka's to the east, with Doren, the forest moon, as the Wooka's home. Its ground is point-symmetric like the others, but its forces aren't: the Wooka come in force, two or three armies to a world against the Thunder Marines' one or two, and the Thunder Marines hold the sky with more ships. On a map that size a side is paid under a third of what its sectors earn, so the war doesn't drown in armies. The short and full maps are point-symmetric in everything, so neither side starts with the better ground.
- **Choosing a fleet:** a war opens before turn 1 with two choices. First pick a fleet from a pool of value (a ship is worth what it costs to build, and so is an army): the *Armada* (mostly ships), the *Battle line* (the map's own mix), the *Vanguard* (the same mix, all on the front line) or the *Horde* (mostly troops), or spend the pool on ships and armies of your own. Then place it on the worlds you hold, with +/- on each world, or let it spread itself. Where it stands is the opening move: a forward fleet hits first but leaves the rear empty, a garrisoned one holds out longest. Both sides place at once and blind, and the AI picks a preset off the campaign's seed. What you don't place goes into the treasury.
- **Bombardment:** hold a world's orbit alone, with their last ship there beaten, and you can train ships on a garrison below: each kills an army before the landings (half as many through a shield generator), and ships that bombard don't sail. It can't take ground by itself: you still have to land to hold it, so winning the war from the sky is possible but slow.
- **Choose a side, walk the phases:** a new campaign asks for the Thunder Marines or the Wooka first. Orders are given in five named phases on a bar along the top of the map, the one you're in lit and the rest greyed: *Muster* (build, cards), *Deploy* (move troops between your own sectors), *Fleets* (ships and bombardments), *Commit* (how many armies land on each enemy sector) and *Battles*. Each phase shows only its own controls and a sentence on what to do; *Next phase* moves on. In Battles every fight shows your odds, or says the garrison is hidden, and you auto-resolve it (the dice settle it) or fight it yourself (one a turn). A recap after each turn gives the odds you had, how it went, and what changed. The bar sorts the orders below; the rules are the same.
- **A turn:** both sides at once, blind to each other's numbers, like a board game of territories and armies. Under the five phases the rules run in six steps. *Draw*: each side draws a card, the same one for both. *Place*: raise troops and ships (armies at a capital, ships at a spaceport), then play cards. *Declare*: move, and any move into the enemy's orbit or ground is an attack. The attacks are declared: each side sees where it's being attacked and from where, but not with how many, and *retreats* if it wants to. A defender can pull everything out of an attacked sector to one it holds on the same world or next door, or pull its ships back along a hyperlane, giving up the ground to keep the troops. An attacker can call an attack off and keep its troops at home. If nobody is attacked, the turn skips straight on. *Combat*: space battles come first, then landings, then ground battles. Each battle you don't play is a cannon barrage of dice: each side fires up to three guns a round, its leader rolling a d8 and the rest d6s, highest against highest, and ties go to the attacker. Pick ⚔ on a sector for its ground battle, or ⚔ on the orbit line for the space battle over the system, and that one is yours instead. *Deploy*: a side that took somewhere moves into it and moves what's left, armies to a sector it holds on the same world or next door and ships to an orbit it holds next door, always leaving one behind. *Collect*: income and region bonuses, and taking three or more places in the turn earns a card. Every die and card comes off the campaign's seed, so an online turn replays the same.
- **Cards:** you hold up to five, and play them in the place phase on somewhere you hold. A *weapon upgrade* (crosshair) arms a sector for good: every die its holder rolls defending it is a d8, and a hero battle there gets an extra turret, until it's taken. A *muster* (shield with a plus) raises armies for free, one for every two you already have on that world: two at least, four at most, so it can't snowball. A *trap* (bomb), set on a sector or orbit for the turn, blows up half of whatever attacks it, and everyone of yours there with it: it's for a place you hold with one army or ship. The enemy never sees your hand or your traps. The AI plays its cards on its front line and its traps under a lone army.
- **Orbit → ground:** the sky sets the landing. Under your own orbit troops land freely, onto any sector if three or more of your ships hold it and onto one new beachhead otherwise. Under an empty sky only four transports get down, into one place. Rush a world through an enemy fleet and each of its ships cuts the landing by one, from three, so a fleet of three seals it, and every two turns it keeps the sky the landing shrinks by one more. The rest turn back, and you fight out of what you took. An enemy orbit blockades a world, so nothing gets built there and supply through it is cut. In the hero battle, your orbit gives you drop-pod reinforcements and orbital strikes, and a fleet of two or more bombards enemy armies every turn. Lose a hero space battle and you feel it at once: the result screen and the log list the armies bombarded below, the blockade, the bombardment to come, and the enemy's troops coming down, often onto a garrison the bombardment already emptied.
- **The muster force:** a side's armies on a world (and the cannons, shields and upgraded guns it holds there) are its muster force, and they feed the space battle over that world: boarding parties go up from it, and the batteries add to a screen or a bombardment. The world's panel shows it on the orbit row (shield with a plus): yours, then theirs, which is a question mark where you have nobody to count it.
- **Ground → orbit:** a shield generator protects its holder's armies from bombardment and cuts the enemy's strikes to one. An anti-ship cannon knocks a ship a turn out of an enemy fleet in its orbit, so a blockade over it doesn't last. On a world with a spaceport, that's where its ships are built. Without the orbit, a side holding a third of a planet or less can't push out from the surface, and has to land again from space.
- **Supply:** supply runs out from your capitals along the hyperlanes, through any system whose orbit the enemy doesn't hold. Armies past an enemy orbit are cut off and lose one a turn, down to a last one holding on.
- **War score:** your share of the key sectors (capitals and spaceports). Hold 75% and you win. Drop under 30% and your income halves, so losing gets worse the longer it goes.
- **The hero battle:** each kind of sector is its own map. Four are in the redwoods of Doren: a shield generator clearing, a capital with five posts ending at the palace, a spaceport's landing pads and control tower, and a cannon battery. Every field is 360 across with four or five strongpoints strung from one spawn to the other, so a battle is a fight over the ground between them, and the side holding more of them bleeds the other's tickets. The trunks go up into the haze, sun comes down through the canopy in shafts with dust hanging in the light, ferns, grass and flowering ground cover are everywhere, over a floor of leaf litter, needles, twigs, pebbles, moss and mushroom clusters, with fallen nurse logs sprouting saplings and dense thickets between the trunks (each kind of sector has its own mix), and a red dirt trail is worn from post to post. The plants sway, and bend away from nearby units and blasts and spring back. It's all for the eye: none of it blocks a shot or a step (`?foliage=low` thins it for a slow machine). The floor rolls, and rises into wooded banks past the edge of the field. Where the Wooka live, there are huts up the trunks, rope bridges between them, and totems wearing the helmets of Marines who came this way before. The dust sea is a desert under two suns: wind-carved sandstone cliffs and hoodoos among dunes, sand blowing, giant ribcages and skulls half buried, ruined moisture farms (domed huts and vaporator towers to fight round) and wrecked haulers, one half-buried by the keep under a tall mast. The jungle is a dense stand of thin trees round a sunken temple, under two layers of canopy, with lianas hanging and swagged between the trunks, buttress roots, moss up the bark, broad-leaf plants, giant ferns, palms and cycads, roots arching over the trail, low mist, and glowing fungi and bioluminescent spots in the gloom. Every map in the woods is strewn with things to hide behind: rocks, stumps and fallen logs. The Wooka have dug in, with trench lines across their half of the field: earth banked up behind a row of sharpened stakes, now and then a skull on one. Trunks, buildings and rock block movement, sight and shots, and a unit standing close behind one, from where the shot comes, is in cover and takes half as many hits from range. Nothing helps against a club at arm's length. Walls lower than eye height give cover but don't block sight. Each post is a power point: a squat bunker whose core glows in its holder's colour. The defender starts holding the last post, their keep, ringed by turrets, which for the Wooka are rock-chuckers. The Marines field four classes of trooper: assault, heavy (rockets that tear up armour), sniper and engineer (who mends vehicles and turrets). Their vehicles come from the map: tanks and speeders with any army, gunships when the side holds the orbit, and walkers when it holds the world's capital. Gunships fly over everything but can't take a post. The Wooka field a horde of five kinds, with their share of the vehicles coming as yet more Wooka, and now and then something worse (below). Fodder are the front line, and at least they are devout. Chubs are bigger, tribal brutes with bigger clubs. Stickies throw spears, then fall back. Boomies run at the thickest knot of the enemy with a lit keg on their backs and go off, hurting their own a little as well, and set each other off. Shanti, the witch doctors, keep behind the line, mend the Wooka round them and throw hexes. The horde hunts in packs: one pack goes up the middle while two swing round the flanks, and when one Wooka is on someone, its pack comes to help. Three on one Thunder Marine tear them apart. Rarely, out of a later surge, comes a Mossback: a Wooka that kept growing, mangy and mossy, long in the arm, running on its knuckles. It leaps the last few metres, swats troopers aside, goes straight through trenches, logs and sandbags, shrugs off rifle fire at a quarter of the damage and mends slowly, unless something heavy (a rocket, a strike, a shell, a Boomie) has hit it in the last few seconds. The radio panics, several lines at once, when one shows up. The whole field is in the fight, not only your corner: both sides' artillery walks shells across it, gunships go past firing, flares go up, trees burn and the smoke rises. Things that die come apart in voxel chunks, blasts throw dirt, and dying Marines get a last word in on the radio. Each side has tickets, and the side holding fewer posts bleeds them. Kill enough of the other side (counted in tickets) and your champion takes the field. For the Wooka it's Warbur, a silverback, half Wooka and half bear, a hunched, tusked troll twice a Chub's size. He spears you from afar, then throws his own Wooka at you (E) until they're too beaten up to throw, then goes for your head: a trooper he gets hold of loses it. A spear that lands by a trunk pins you to it, and so do a Sticky's. For the Thunder Marines it's Gumpert, who swaps kit with E: a rocket launcher, a stun baton, or a jetpack that carries him over everything. If the hero is on that side, they become the champion; otherwise one walks in at the spawn. The hero's blaster heats as it fires and locks up for a moment if it's run dry, and running straight ahead without firing breaks into a sprint. Hits flash a marker on the crosshair, taking one reddens the edge of the screen, nearby blasts shake the camera, and kills in a row get a toast. Blaster fire is glowing bolts you can follow, red for the Thunder Marines (the Wooka's rock-chuckers throw an amber blur), with a flash at the muzzle and a spark where they land. Your hero has three lives. Their level carries over between battles, and losing all three lives sidelines them for three turns. You can also leave a battle to the AI from the briefing, or partway through.
- **Match modes:** a ground battle is fought one of five ways. Conquest is the war over every post at once. Assault opens one post at a time, in order: take it, hold it for 40 seconds while they counter-attack, and the next comes live, till the keep falls or the clock runs out. King of the hill puts one live post in the middle of the field and moves it on every 75 seconds, and whoever stands on it drains the other side. Hold-out puts turrets on every post and sends five minutes of waves that keep growing at the defender. Escort puts the attacker's champion on the field from the start, to be walked to the keep: if the hero is attacking, that's you, and an AI champion only moves with a friend close by. Lose the champion and you lose. The HUD says what the mode wants from you right now, and a mission card in the corner gives you one job: defend a post your side holds, take one it doesn't, walk the champion in or stop them. A waypoint over the objective (at the screen's edge, pointing, when it's off screen) counts down the metres while you make it there. Once you're in the area, step out of it and a red banner warns you you're leaving; when the objective changes, a chime and a note tell you where to go next. Every mode has the same rhythm, strongest in hold-out and assault. Surges of Wooka pour out of the treeline in waves (a siren icon and a ticking clock count down to the next). Between them the radio calls a dig-in (a shovel): hold a post you own with two of yours, and a drop of troops and tickets comes, then an orbital strike on the next. When a post is overrun, with the enemy well past your side round it for a few seconds, the radio calls a fall-back (an exit icon) to the next post, or an evac point if there is none, with a short window. Get out in time and it costs a few tickets; be left behind and it costs a lot.
- **Your squad:** 1 to 4 give orders to everyone of yours within 60 m: *forward* to where your gun points, *hold* where they stand, *fall back* towards home, or *focus fire* on whatever is round the spot your gun points at (F works too). An order lasts 40 seconds, then they go back to fighting their own way, and a bar along the bottom shows which is in force. Orders go in the input log, so a played battle replays them.
- **Heavy weapons:** three are left out in the open between the lines, under a beacon you can see across the field: a rail lance that goes through everyone on its line, a plasma mortar that lobs a shell where you aim, and a repeater that hoses heavy rounds (for the Wooka: a thunder spear, a keg sling and a rock-chucker). Walk over one to pick it up. It fires till its ammo runs out and never overheats, and the crate restocks a minute after it's taken. You drop it when you go down.
- **The HUD:** the Thunder Marines' is black plates with gold rules and clipped corners, Roman capitals and stamped numerals. The Wooka's is hide and bark with lashed edges and daubed lettering. The blaster's heat is an arc of ten segments under the crosshair that goes yellow, amber, then red, warns you near the top, and drains as it vents once it's locked (with a hiss); with a heavy weapon in hand the arc counts its ammo. Your comrades talk in bubbles over their heads: something daft when it's quiet, something worried when it isn't. The radio, the last words and the cutscenes lean on deadpan disaster-movie gags (the bones, a very small bite, running away, charging, running away again).
- **Big maps:** five kinds of sector have a big map too, half as wide again with a post or two more: Generator ridge, Old city outskirts, Cargo yards, Deep gorge and Temple valley. A battle with six or more armies between the two sides is fought on one.
- **Cutscenes:** a battle opens with one, once per browser (`?cutscenes=always` to see it every time, `?cutscenes=off` for never). The Thunder Marines drop in first: the saga's title card over a dropship that is mostly flying, two supply crates kicked out of it, one chute that opens and one crate that had the snacks. Then the briefing: they meet one knee-high Wooka on a stump, and nobody is worried until it goes for Hale. The Wooka hold a war council: the chieftain yells, the kin go wild, and a Chub throws a little one fifteen metres for the joy of it. The first Mossback of a battle gets a short sting. Space, Enter, Esc or a click skips. A cutscene is a list of cues in `cutscenes.ts` (put an actor down, walk it, have one throw another, a speech bubble, a caption, a title card, a camera move, a shake, a sound), so a new one is just more data.
- **Sound:** made in the page with Web Audio, no files. Blasters pew, vehicle cannons boom lower, blasts crunch over a thump and orbital strikes rumble on. The Wooka sound like gorillas crossed with howler monkeys: low, rough hoots and barks, a pulsing roar that carries across the forest (the bigger the Wooka, the deeper, down to the Mossback) and a thump on the chest, with a whoop when they charge or get thrown and a scream when they go down. A Wooka rears up and beats its chest in time with its roar, and the Thunder Marines grunt through their masks. Every sound is placed: quieter with distance, panned to the side it came from. The space battle gets the same bolts and blasts. M mutes, and it's remembered.
- **The end-of-match vote:** after a ground battle, everyone votes once for how the next one is fought, and every voter's pointer shows on the vote screen as they move it. Against the AI, its pointer wanders over to a card and clicks. In hotseat, the one pointer votes for each side in turn. Online, the other seat's pointer comes over a WebSocket on the campaign's Durable Object. A split vote goes one way by the match id, the same on both screens. A quick-play battle ends in the same vote, and the pick is the rematch.
- **On a phone:** the war table reflows to one column, and both battles get on-screen controls on a touch screen: a stick to move, a drag on the field to look (ground) or steer (space), and buttons to fire, call a strike, use a vehicle or special, and give the squad orders (boost and brake in space). `?touch=1` turns them on from a desktop browser.
- **Controls:** the gear in the corner of every screen opens a panel: invert look up/down and left/right, mouse and touch look sensitivity, field of view, click-to-toggle firing, left-handed touch layout, and rebindable keys (click a row, press a key; clashes are refused). Changes apply mid-battle, are saved in the browser, and Reset puts the defaults back (`src/settings.ts`).
- **The space battle:** fought by picking, by default. Four rounds, and each round both sides pick one order from a hand of five, seeing the other's only once theirs is in: *broadside* beats boarders and a fleet bombarding, a fighter *screen* beats a broadside and a bombardment, *board* beats a screen and a bombardment and takes prize ships, *bombard* kills armies on the world below but takes every hit, and *pull back* takes half the fire, gives none and comes back harder. No order twice running, so leaning on one gets read. Every hit scales with the fleet's hull, boarding needs troops from the muster force (and a muster of four or more boards harder), and more hull after the last round holds the orbit, the defender on a tie. The enemy's picks come from the AI, which plays either side the same way. A round takes a few seconds, but a bad run loses the orbit. The result carries the hero's picks, and the table fights it again from them.
- **Flying it:** *Fly it* on the briefing puts you in the fighter battle instead, played in 2.5D, looking down at an angle on Doren's orbit, with a faint grid on the battle's plane and a line dropped from each capital ship to it. The ships are built from voxels, like a toy village. Each ship on the map is a capital ship over the planet, launching its wing of fighters. The Marines fly long stepped wedges, black with white trim, rows of lit windows and a command tower astern, with black voxel darts for fighters. The Wooka fly villages on rafts of lashed logs, with huts, a tree, patched hide sails and a carved Wooka head on the bow, and launch hollowed-out logs with hide sails. Cruisers trade broadsides across the gap and shoot at fighters that come close. A third of the fighters fly bombing runs on the enemy cruisers and the rest dogfight. Lose every cruiser and you lose the orbit. At the time limit, the side with more hull left keeps it. Afterwards a cruiser under half its hull counts as lost, and then the turn's landings come down under whoever holds the orbit. The hero flies a fighter of their own, with three lives, like on the ground. A hero who loses the orbit goes down with the flagship and is out six turns. A side with armies on a world but no ships over it can send its hero to break out: one ship against the whole blockade, which is very hard, and the same for either side.

## Run it

```
cd apps/two-fronts
bun install
bun run dev              # in the browser
bun run desktop:dev      # as a desktop app
bun test test            # the rules, fleets, the AI, the turn sequencing, the campaign's Durable Object and both battle sims, headless
bun run edge:dev         # the page plus the Worker and its Durable Objects in local workerd, for online campaigns
```

`bun run dev` alongside `bunx wrangler dev` works too: Vite sends `/api` on to the Worker. `bun run deploy` ships it to Cloudflare. That's the owner's call, as it's a live Worker with Durable Objects on the account.

In a ground battle: click the field to take control, WASD to move, the mouse to look, click to fire, Q for an orbital strike where you're aiming, E to get in or out of a vehicle (as Warbur, to throw a Wooka; as Gumpert, to swap kit), 1 to 4 to order your squad, M to mute, and Esc to let go of the mouse.

In a space battle: your fighter turns towards the mouse. Hold the button to fire, W to boost, S to brake, and the wheel to zoom.

## How it's built

- `src/battle/contract.ts`: `BattleSpec` (what the map gives the battle) and `BattleResult` (what comes back), and `SpaceBattleSpec` and `SpaceBattleResult` for a fight over the orbit. It's the only file both layers import.
- `src/war/`: the strategy layer. It's a deterministic rules engine over plain JSON (`rules.ts`), the three maps (`galaxy.ts`, picked per campaign) and the AI (`ai.ts`). `declareAttacks` turns a turn's moves into the attacks each side is told about, and `diceBattle` is the cannon barrage. `checkRetreats` checks a side's answer to them, and `fogged` is what a side sees of the map. The table holds the moves out of sight while the retreats come in, and the AI answers for its own side (`aiRetreats`). `resolveTurn` pauses on the hero battle's spec. `applyBattleResult` finishes the turn after a ground battle. After a space battle, `applySpaceResult` runs the landings and the ground battles still to come. A turn where somebody took somewhere pauses again for the deploys (`planDeploys` checks them, the AI's come from `aiDeploys`), and `deployTurn` finishes it.
- `src/battle/sim.ts`: the ground battle as a pure fixed-step simulation, scenery and sight lines included. The tests run it from a fixture spec with no strategy layer and no renderer. `view.ts` draws it in three.js and turns the keyboard and mouse into the hero's input. `models.ts` builds the Thunder Marines, the Wooka, the vehicles, the woods and the ships from three.js primitives and textures drawn on a canvas, so there are no assets to load or license. Each model is a rig of named joints, its parts merged into a mesh or two per joint (colour, roughness and metalness ride on the vertices), built once and cloned. A Thunder Marine is about two dozen draw calls and a Wooka about ten. `animate` poses a clone each frame from what its unit is doing: walk cycles, the Wooka's waddle and club swing, recoil, wobbling antennas, a walker's stride. With `bun run dev`, `/lineup.html` puts every model on a turntable (`?row=marines|wooka|vehicles|space|cruisers`, `&walk=1` to walk them).
- `scripts/balance.ts`, `scripts/sim.ts`, `scripts/sim-worker.ts`: the balance tools. `balance.ts` plays every sector both ways round and prints who won; `sim.ts` plays thousands of battles on worker threads, capped at 20% of the cores at the lowest priority, and writes a report with a heatmap and a replay you can scrub (`bun scripts/sim.ts --n 1000 --hero both --handicap 0,0.3,0.6`, then open `dist/balance/report.html`). `--handicap` with no hero gives the attacker that much more (the force-edge check), `--orders` has the scripted player lead its squad. `src/battle/handicap.ts` is the one place the human handicap lives: the AI side fields about 45% more when a human leads the other, and `buildSpec` applies it. `test/balance.test.ts` pins AI against AI roughly even on every sector, the defender winning about 60% at equal forces, and the handicap.
- `src/battle/terrain.ts`: the world around a ground battle, for the eye only. It lays the floor, the trees and their boughs, the undergrowth, the sun shafts and motes, the Wooka's huts and totems, and dresses the sim's blocks as sandbags, bunkers or rock. The sim stays a flat plain: `heightAt` lifts what's drawn onto the rolling floor, and none of it changes a shot or a step, so replays still match.
- `src/battle/cutscene.ts`: cutscenes as data, a list of cues on a clock, and pure functions for where each actor and the camera are at any moment and what words are on screen. `cutscenes.ts` holds the scenes, `cutscene-view.ts` plays one in the battle's scene at an origin on the field, under letterbox bars, while the sim waits.
- `src/battle/space-picks.ts`: the pick battle, a pure function of the spec, its seed and the picks. The AI's picker (`aiSpacePick` in `ai.ts`) is handed in, so the battle layer still imports nothing from the war.
- `src/battle/space.ts`: the flown space battle, again a pure fixed-step simulation tested headless. `space-view.ts` draws it in 2.5D: an angled camera from behind your own line that follows the hero's fighter, which steers towards the point on its plane under the mouse.
- `src/battle/replay.ts`: the hero's input, a step at a time, as a log of about 10 to 50 KB that a played result carries. Both sims take their trig from `fmath.ts` (only + − × ÷ and √, which every engine rounds alike), so a log plays back to the same result in any browser, Bun or a Worker.
- `src/war/table.ts`: who sits in each seat and the orders handed in this turn. The last human's orders resolve the turn, and the AI's seat gives its orders then. A hero battle, on the ground or in orbit, holds the turn for that hero's side. It only stores and sequences the pure rules, so the page and the Durable Object run the same code. A result's numbers are never taken at its word: a battle left to the AI is run again, and a played one is played again from its input log, and that run's result is the one kept.
- `src/ui/vote.ts`: the end-of-match vote screen. The vote itself is on the table (`castVote` in `table.ts`), so the page and the Durable Object count it the same way, and the picked mode goes into the next `BattleSpec`.
- `src/edge/`: the Worker (`worker.ts`) and `CampaignRoom`, one Durable Object per campaign. Its SQLite holds the table as one row and every resolved turn as another (`turns`: the orders, the hero battle's result, the log). Each human side gets a secret seat token when the campaign starts. The link carries the token in its `#fragment`, and the object keeps only its hash. A seat sees the map through its own fog of war and who's still to move, never the other side's orders before the turn resolves. While the retreats are out, it sees the declared attacks but not their numbers. `GET /api/campaigns/<id>/history` reads the turns back.
- `src/main.ts`: the map, the orders panel, and the screens between them. A game against the AI or hotseat is saved in the browser's localStorage. An online game lives in its Durable Object (`src/net.ts` calls it).

## Editing the script

The game's words (radio, chatter, cutscenes and places) live in `content/*.json`. `bun run cms` serves them as textareas on localhost and saves back to the files; reload the game to see them.
