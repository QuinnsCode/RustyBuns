# Sound Guy Simulator

> **What it proves:** a first-person roadie sim on real numbers: you carry the gear in one piece at a time, at a pace set by its weight (a 40 kg cab is a shuffle), and set it down on the floor or stacked on another piece. Cables come out of the road case as rolls at their real weight: F at the case lifts a roll into your hands, F at a jack plugs its end and you keep the roll, Space winds up and bowls it (it pays out cable as it rolls, a heavy one stops sooner), and F picks it up where it stopped to carry the rest to its jack, where the spare stays coiled. A short power run is a 0.17 kg/m cord, 20 m and up a 1.1 kg/m feeder; what you carry and drag slows your walk, and your hands show it (a coil over the fingers, a fist round the cable, a plug at a lit jack). A cable across a walkway is a trip hazard until a ramp goes over it. Speakers first; the plan is in #4849 and `notes/game-ideas/3-studio-and-roadie.md`.

Until 2026-11-17 this was Load In; the folder, the `loadIn` handle and the deploy keep that name. The build order is #5029.

**The cable feel test** (`feel.html`: `bun run dev`, then `/feel.html`): one room, the game's own sound board, amp rack, wedge and mic stand models (jacks where the models put them), the game's hands, and four rolls of cable at their real weight (mic 0.06, speaker 0.21, feeder 1.1 kg/m). Plug a roll's end in, bowl the roll and it pays out behind it: a 16.5 kg feeder dies in a few metres, a mic cable runs the room. Dragging laid cable slows you by what it weighs. The sim is `src/cables/roll.ts`; `bun apps/load-in/scripts/feel-journey.ts` plays the whole room through headless and fails if the wedge does not come up; `window.feel` plays it without a mouse (`bun run probe load-in /feel.html --js "feel.state()"`).

**Your ink** (`src/you/`): each rung of the tour you pass puts a flash tattoo on a forearm, and the Ink scrap on the menu takes one picture of your own (kept in this browser only). Hold Y to look at your arms.

**A live set** (`src/crew/`, `src/audio/decks.ts`): in a crew room one player at a time has the DJ seat, and the decks are shared, so every page plays the same mix in step and each player hears it from where they stand. A late joiner lands in time; a handover does not restart the track.

**Dust** (the desert, `src/venues/desert.ts`): a plugged joint lying within 20 cm of the sand fills with dust by sunrise and crackles. Stand the gear on a case or the cart, or tape the joint (G).

## Run it

```
bun install
bun run dev              # in the browser
bun run desktop:dev      # as the desktop app
bun test                 # the garage load-in and a power run, played by the tests
```

Click to take the mouse. WASD walk, mouse look, F (or a click) picks up, puts down, takes a cable from the case, plugs
it into the jack you look at or pulls it out; Q / E turn what you hold, P switch on what you look at, M speakers up or
down, C crouch, Esc lets go. A speaker with no cable in it plays once switched on; once cabled, it plays only when the
patch feeds it.
The dial under Play goes from **Cozy** (no clock, gear weighs half, a cable you let go of coils itself, ramps snap end to end, free jacks glow when you hold a cable, breakers never trip) through **Crew** to **Sim** (real weights, doors on time, breakers trip, a short cable is short). A new player starts on Crew, and the browser remembers the stop. With a clock, the doors time is at the top of the screen. Each minute late, after Crew's two minutes of grace, costs 10% of the pay and 8 points of crowd mood, and the mood scales the show score when the doors open.

The first gig (`?level=first-gig`), the first rung of the tour: a small room, a singer, and you wire up a sound board. One condenser mic on a stand, one desk at the back of the room, one amp rack and one passive wedge, in the order a crew does it: the rack by the stage (an XLR run loses nothing over 15 m, a speaker run loses level and the amp's grip on the cone, so short speaker run, long signal run), power to the rack and the desk, the mic into the desk with 48 V on (V at the desk; a condenser with no phantom makes no sound), the desk's aux out to the amp, the amp to the wedge, then the desk on and the amp last (and off first), or the wedge thumps. The card ticks each job off and reads the level at the singer's head; Enter is doors.

The dive bar (`?level=dive-bar`): set the tops on their stands, then, looking at one with your hands free, Q / E turn it, R / V tilt it, Z / X lower or raise its stand. H shows the coverage heat map, [ and ] move the master fader, and Enter opens the doors on your score. The crowd area should sit within 4 dB of 100 dB, with the bar at least 5 dB quieter so the staff can hear orders. The card also shows the score with the room full: the crowd (the house plus your following on tour, the full room on `?level=`) packs at the stage and soaks up the highs on any line through their heads, so tops at 1.2 m that pass at soundcheck lose the back once the doors open. Raise them over the heads and give them a little more fader. The full room also rings shorter, the crowd makes its own noise, and you walk slower through it. Each part of the crowd dances, drifts forward, leaves or thins out at the front, depending on what it hears (`state().crowd`).

The bigger bar (`?level=bigger-bar`): passive 15" tops and two subs run off the amp rack over speaker cable, two powered wedges, and wall outlets on four numbered circuits (two 20 A on the stage, 15 A at the bar and the side). The cables decide what plays. The bar's cooler is always on circuit 3, so the rack on that circuit trips it at show level. Walk to the panel on the right wall and press B to reset it, then move the plug. The card shows each circuit and how many speakers are fed; Enter opens the doors on the share of speakers fed (warm drinks, from a tripped cooler, cost 30).

The park (`?level=park`): a Saturday event with a pop-up tent and no wall power. F on the generator (90 kg, too heavy to lift) takes its handle and you push it in from the truck at a heavy pace; run the 25 m power cable to the distro in the tent and the short ones to the tops and the mixer, all inside the generator's 6.3 kW (past it, it trips and the park goes quiet). It comes with 4 litres in its tank, not enough for the 8-hour day: carry a jerry can from the truck (18 kg, one at a time) and F on the generator pours it in. The card shows the hours left at the load you've plugged in (more draw burns faster), and if the fuel runs out before the day ends, the score drops by the share of the day the park sits quiet. Cables across the lakeside path or the dog walk need a ramp, and the houses across the road have a noise limit of 82 dB at their property line: aim, tilt and the fader trade the crowd's 95 dB against the neighbours. On Cozy the generator rolls light and never trips, and ramps snap over the runs. Same keys as the dive bar; Enter is show time.
The big arena (`?level=arena`): the PA flies. Sixteen 75 kg line-array boxes and four subs wait on the dock, and the boxes are too heavy for one person (on Cozy they weigh half, so you can), so four hired hands carry what you send them, two to a box. Press 1 to 6 to pick a rigging point (the two mains, the two side hangs, the two delay towers halfway down the floor), then K on a box sends the crew with it to that point's fly bar; K on anything else tags it, and K again sends it to where you stand. At a point's motor, U flies the hang to trim and R / V angle it. , and . step a tower's delay: it should land just after the mains (0 to 20 ms), about 65 ms for these towers. The subs go to the stage edge in cardioid pairs: a second sub 0.8 m behind the first, facing the same way, with its polarity flipped (I) and its delay stepped with , and . to the time sound takes to cross the gap (2.3 ms), so the bass cancels on the band's stage and adds out front. Power is the building's company switch on the right wall: 7 picks the sub amps, and L there moves the picked feed onto the next of three 50 A legs. Everything on one leg trips it. The card scores the floor at 102 dB, the towers in time, the stage 10 dB quieter in the lows, and every leg under 80%.
The small arena (`?level=small-arena`): the first room where speakers hang. Four rigging points in the roof steel, two for the main line arrays (350 kg each) and two for the out-fills that turn to the side seats (170 kg). Carry a line array box (36 kg) from the dock to under a point and press G to pin it under the hang; K sets the pins from the splay calculator for the trim you plan (U / J move the plan), and I is the rigger's check, the boxes, the frame and the motor's chain against the steel's limit (eight boxes fit a main point, nine don't). The motor (O) runs only on a checked point and only with you at least 2.5 m clear, at 4 m a minute on the clock. Front fills go on the stage lip for the front rows the arrays shoot over, and the desk goes out at front of house, where the fader is. H shows the coverage map in 3D, a tile at every seat on the rake; the score weighs the floor, the end seats, the side seats and the front rows, each within 4 dB of 100 dB, and passes at 85 with the desk out.
**The tour.** Play takes you to the gig you're booked at, up a ladder that scales to a real arena rig: the first gig, the dive bar, the bigger bar, the community event in the park, the desert night party, the small arena and the arena (a local festival and a red-rock amphitheatre join as they're built). The card shows each rung's whole rig as a crew would build it for that crowd: tops, subs, wedges, amp channels, kW and the power it runs on, cable runs and crew, from one wedge on a wall circuit to 76 flown boxes, 24 subs and 250 kW of three-phase. Your following is the crowd your good nights build: it fills the next room on top of the house's own crowd (never past what the room holds), the fee is what a full room pays, and a poor night loses a fifth of it. Home base, in the menu, is the cash, the truck and the shop. Each gig needs its kit, the gear its venue starts with; what you don't own you rent for the night at 5% of its price, and if the kit outweighs your truck (the hatchback carries 150 kg, the van 600, the box truck 2000) you rent the next one up at 10%. Doors settle the night: the pay less the rent goes in the bank, a show that passes books the next rung, and one that doesn't books you back at the same place. Buy gear and a bigger truck to keep more of the pay. The tour is kept in this browser; a page opened with `?level=` plays that level and leaves the tour alone.
The show (`?level=show`): the rig is up and the band is on stage; you run front of house. At the desk, 1 to 5 pick a channel (kick, snare, bass, guitar, vocal), [ ] move its fader, ; ' its send to the singer's wedge, , . its trim, J / K move the notch finder a third of an octave and N puts a notch there (three per channel). Enter opens the doors, or the clock does after two minutes. Three songs of our own play as stems through the desk, and the crowd meter climbs while every part sits within 3 dB of where the song wants it (the guitar 5 dB up for the solo in the second song). Feedback is computed: the vocal mic hears the wedge at their distance and angle, through the channel's trim, send and notches, and a band whose loop gain passes 0 dB rings. "More me" asks for 3 dB more in the wedge, which rings at 5 kHz unless you notch it or turn the wedge (Q / E beside it). A broken string (F by the guitarist hands up the spare) and a dying bass cable (F at the bass rig) cut that part until you fix them. Cozy names the ringing band and the fix and gives the band a minute; Sim gives 25 seconds and you find the ring by ear.
The desert (`?level=desert`): a night party out past the end of the road, under a full moon. Every box walks 30 m over sand from the truck, and sand slows every step. The generator's small wheels dig in, so it rides the sand cart: F on the cart pushes both. The wall is four horn-loaded dance stacks, each a 115 kg 21" bin with a mid-bass horn and a mid-high horn on it, off two amp racks. At 235 kg a stack is past anyone's lift, so each comes off the tail lift strapped on a sand dolly: F on the dolly pushes the whole stack, at a crawl. Bins shoulder to shoulder sum on the whole dance floor (115 dB at 63 Hz). Split them into two stacks a side, or stand every other one 2.7 m back, and parts of the floor lose the bass. The floor's mids and highs are judged through the night's peak crowd, so the mid-highs stay up on the stack where they clear the heads, and a fader that sounds right on the empty sand at dusk is too quiet once the floor fills. B shows the bass map, H the mids and highs. The horns beam, so the crew's tents out front on the right (limit 85 dB) sleep unless a stack is turned their way. The night runs twelve hours on what is in the generator (it arrives with 15 L) plus any jerry can poured in or set down beside it. On its tank alone the wall goes quiet at about 3 am, so the night doesn't pass. Enter is show time: the sky runs to sunrise, or goes dark when the fuel runs out.

The red-rock amphitheatre (`?level=amphitheatre`): the top venue. An open-air bowl between two great slanted sandstone walls at 1,950 m, the stage at the bottom under a roof, seventy rows of benches climbing 30 m over 100 m (you climb them), a third rock behind the stage and houses 1.5 km down the valley. At a point's controller in the wing, U / J has the crew pin boxes on (the roof takes 1,100 kg a point: thirteen 75 kg boxes, not fourteen), K sets the pins for the rake from row 70 to row 1, < > toe the hang in off the rock, N swaps the horns to 70 degrees, B shelves the long-throw third up at 8 kHz (dry thin air takes over 20 dB of the top octave in 100 m), I checks and O flies. At row 36, G has the crew put the delay towers up; U / J times them (the path from the mains over the speed of sound at the show's temperature, plus 8 ms so the mains still lead). V at the lip turns the subs cardioid, so the stage and the back rock don't boom and the bass doesn't carry to the houses. H maps the bowl. The score: even from row 1 to 70, highs at the top against the desk's, no echo off the rock or out-of-time towers, the houses under 60 dB, and a quiet stage, under 103 dB at the desk; a late doors cuts the set at the hard curfew.

Strike and load out, at every venue: the truck is parked outside with its back open (at the arenas it is backed up to the dock, a trailer at the big one; at the desert it waits at the end of the track, with the curfew at sunrise; after the show the whole night's rig comes down). L starts the load-out and the clock counts to curfew instead of doors. Unplug every cable, carry an end to the case and press F to coil it, then Q (over) and E (under) a loop at a time: alternate them, because each loop that goes the same way as the one before is a twist, 15 seconds of untangling the next time that cable comes out. Pack every piece into the truck front to back: set down over the bed, a piece is shoved forward to meet the bulkhead or whatever is in front of it, and one with more than an arm's shove of gap in front will slide on the drive, as will anything on top of a piece that slides; a heavy piece on a lighter one crushes it. At the arenas the hangs come down first: the truck won't shut while a box is still up on a hang, and in the big arena the crew carries each box into the truck where you send it. Step out and press T to shut the truck. The card tallies the time, the minutes past curfew and the venue's fine for each, what slid and the twists. Your own garage keeps no curfew, and on Cozy cables coil themselves flat.
A gamepad works too (left stick walk, right stick look, A grab, LB / RB turn, B crouch); the cog rebinds it.
On a phone or tablet, a thumb on the left half of the screen is a stick to walk and a drag on the right half looks. The
buttons bottom right press the keys: grab, turn, crouch (it stays down until you tap it again), switch, strike and the
truck, with the venue's own keys over them (aim, stand, fader, heat map, doors, the arena's points and the desk).

## Sign-in and parties

The page plays as a guest until eqosys knows the game. To turn sign-in on:

1. On [eqosys](https://eqosys.qntbr.com), open Studio, then **Add a game**: id `load-in`, and your site's address.
2. Put the same address in `SIGN_IN_FROM` in `src/eqosys.ts`, and deploy (`bun run deploy`).

Signed in, **Make a party** gives a code to hand out; friends type it in **Join**. The host's **Start** seats everyone
(`state().party.seat`) and puts everyone in one crew room on the level the host is on (`?room=CODE` in the address does
the same). You see each other, and gear one of you moves is moved for everyone. Anything 30 kg or more (a sub, the bar's
cooler) takes two: F on it takes one end, and your friend's F on it takes the other. It comes up between you at
the pace of half its weight, and neither of you can walk off from the other, so you walk it the same way. Over 50 kg one
person can't lift it at all. Light gear someone holds is theirs until they set it down. Cables are shared too: one of
you takes a cable out of the case to the wall while another walks its far end to the speaker and lays the ramp, and
everyone sees the same patch. A cable end in a friend's hand is theirs until they let go of it. Switches are still each
player's own for now.

The crew room is a World Durable Object per level and code (`src/edge/`); `bun run edge:dev` runs it locally behind
the built page. Plain `bun run dev` has no `/ws`, so a party plays alone there.

## The harness

`window.loadIn.state()` returns you (place, facing, speed), what you hold, what F would pick up, and every piece of gear
(where it sits, its weight), the cables (ends, slack), the hazards and the patch (`fed`, `problems`), and `sound`: each speaker's level at your head, the high and low band totals. In the show, `state().show` is the set (song, solo, crowd, open, fixed and missed cues), `ringing` and `margin` for the vocal loop, `me` and `wants` for the wedge, the mix and the desk; `desk.nudge(channel, { fader, send, trim })`, `desk.notch(channel, hz)`, `desk.start()`, `desk.fix(kind)` and `desk.run(seconds)` play it. `state().tour` is the cash, truck, booked gig, nights played and whether tonight is settled; `tour()` is the whole tour and `buy(id)` buys gear or a truck. `power(key)` switches gear on, `speakers(up)` brings the sound up. `start()`, `walkTo(x, z)`, `pickUp(key)`, `putDown()`, `turn(dir)` and `look(yaw, pitch)`
play it without pointer lock, and `takeCable`, `plug`, `unplug`, `grabEnd`, `dropEnd`, `coil`, `loop`, `coilAll` and `wait` run the cables. `strike()` starts the load-out, `state().strike` says what is left (curfew, out, sliding, crushing, cables, twists), and `shut()` closes the truck with its tally. In the Rusty Buns repo, `bun run probe load-in` reads it from headless Chrome.
When the game grows, grow `state()` with it, and its row in `HANDLES` (`scripts/probe.ts`).
