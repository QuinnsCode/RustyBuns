## Use it

**What it is:** a music maker in the browser: drums and synths you play, record and loop.

**Use:** https://fm-daw.notryanquinn.workers.dev

**How to use:** press Space to play the beat, pick a track, and play notes on your keyboard.
Press R to record what you play; click steps on the grid to add notes by hand.
**Song mode:** the list button beside Bars turns the loop into pattern A. Use + to add another copy of the last slot, the star to add a new pattern, and − to drop the last slot. Click a slot's letter to make it play the next pattern (A to H). A note you draw in one A shows up in every A. Patterns are what their first slot holds, so taking the last copy of a pattern out of the chain lets it go; undo brings it back. Automation follows the patterns too: a filter sweep drawn (or recorded) in one A plays in every A, and so do Shape's moves on the master. A point right on a slot's end belongs to the next slot. Audio clips stay on the timeline. The list button again leaves song mode and keeps the notes where they are.
To put something on a track, use the four buttons over the grid: **Record audio** (a mic or guitar, one pass of the loop), **Record MIDI** (the notes you play), **Import audio** (wav, mp3, m4a, ogg, flac) and **Import MIDI** (a .mid file). You can also drop a file on a track.
The piano roll has four tools: the pencil (B) draws (drag on empty space: the start lands on the Grid, the end where you let go), the eraser (E) takes off every note you click or drag across, the scissors (C) open a note's popover with a thumb for its start and one for its end (drag, touch-drag or scroll over a thumb; Esc closes it), and the hand (H) moves a note anywhere, off the Grid. Snap (N, the magnet) keeps the pencil's start and the thumbs on the Grid, and Alt frees one drag.
Recorded or imported audio sits as clips in the lane under the grid: drag a clip's edges to trim it, Alt-drag to slide the sound under it, and C cuts it in two.
**Shape** (the tab, or the Shape button on any Mixer strip) is the EQ, on every track, player, return and the master.
**DJ decks:** `/decks.html` (or click anywhere on the DJ table in a jam room) opens both decks and the mixer at once. Bring music with **Add music** (files stay on your computer), the upload button on a deck or on its lane of the close-up (A on top, B under it), **Folder** for a whole folder in the library, or paste a YouTube link and press **A** or **B** to play it on that deck (Enter, or a paste anywhere on the decks, puts it in the library and on an empty deck; with both decks in use it waits in the library). YouTube links stay in the library for next time. On a phone, swipe between deck A, the mixer and deck B, or press their tabs.

**Take your set out:** the red dot in the decks' header records the master to a file on your computer with a track list. The radio button beside it is your **listen link**: name the set, press **Go live**, and send the link. Anyone who opens it presses Play and hears your master a few seconds behind, with the track list as each track comes in; no sign-in. **Stop** ends it for everyone. Nothing is sent until you go live, and YouTube decks aren't in it (they play in YouTube's own player). The link is the same each time from your browser. Going live needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox, and the decks online (not the desktop app).

**Light or dark:** the room pill at the top right of every page (the one that names where you are) ends in three icons: follow your computer, light, or dark. The pick is kept in this browser and every page reads it; the 3D rooms, the field at dusk, the screens and the gear keep their own colours.

**Speaker plans:** `/speakers.html` builds a speaker box from its plan: pick one of the three, drag to turn the cabinet, **Apart** pulls the pieces out like a build sheet, and the curve, the walls and the cut list are worked out from the plan. Change what the walls are made of or add braces and listen: **Listen** plays a bass loop through the box (in Rust when this build has the wasm), **No box** the same loop without it, and **Knock** rings each wall on its own: MDF thuds, aluminium sings. **GLB** downloads the cabinet as it stands, to open in Blender.

**Controls:** Desktop: Space play and stop, the A row plays notes, Z X change octave, R record, 1 to 0 pick a track, - and = zoom the grid out and in, \\ fits the whole loop, Esc stop all sound. Ctrl or Cmd + scroll (or a trackpad pinch) zooms about the pointer, a sideways or Shift scroll slides. · Phone: tap the pads and buttons; pinch the grid or the overview bar above it to zoom.

**Account:** not needed. Sign in with eqosys to jam in a room with friends. Opened a friend's invite link without an account? **Join the room** opens eqosys in a small window beside it: make an account there (a name, an email, a password) and you land back in the same room, signed in.

A small FM groovebox: twelve tracks (five drums and seven synths, each FM or analog), a drum machine (grooves and kits), a reverb and delay called Space with a send on every track, an EQ called Shape on every channel (the master, each track, each player and both returns), automation lanes, a step sequencer and a piano roll, live recording from the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard, and quantize. The sound engine is written twice, once in Rust and once in TypeScript, and you can switch between them while the groove plays.

## Run it

Run every command from the folder named in its step.

1. **Install** (repo root, once, and again after pulling new dependencies):
   ```
   bun install
   ```
2. **Go to the app:**
   ```
   cd apps/fm-daw
   ```
3. **Optional, for the Rust sound engine** (once per machine; needs [rustup](https://rustup.rs)):
   ```
   rustup target add wasm32-unknown-unknown
   bun run build:native
   ```
   Skip this and the app uses the TypeScript engine only. It still works.
4. **Build and launch the desktop app:**
   ```
   bun run desktop:dev
   ```
   This is `bunx rustybuns build desktop --dev --check && bunx rustybuns run desktop`. Turn your speakers down first.

Ship a binary for this machine (about 62 MB on macOS arm64, with the Rust engine inside):

```
bun run desktop:build           # dist/fm-daw-<os>-<arch>
```

### Plain browser with hot reload

`bun run build` makes the whole web app in `dist/client`: it builds the Rust engine to wasm (skipped with a note if cargo or the wasm32 target is missing; the TypeScript engine runs instead), writes the `.rustybuns/vite.ts` boundary plugin (`bunx rustybuns boundary`), then runs Vite. Run it once before `bun run dev`; if you see `Could not resolve "./.rustybuns/vite"`, that's why. Without a world (plain `vite dev`) nothing saves, and bounces download instead.

Jump to a song from the address: `?song=wobble-lab` opens Wobble Lab (also `demo`, `night-shift`, `long-trail`) solo, so it never replaces a sesh's groove, and `&track=6` selects that track (numbered as on screen) with its Perform panel. `bun run manual` opens #2636 this way.

To try the Cloudflare version on your machine, seshes and all: `bun run build`, then `bunx wrangler dev`, open `http://localhost:8787/desk` (it sends you to a new `/sesh/<name>`; `/` opens the front door), and open that address in a second browser. `--var ROOM_SEATS:1` shows the full-room card.

### Jam in a sesh (online)

- **A sesh is a named room:** a visit to `/desk` gets a new name, like `/sesh/fuzzy-black-panda`. There are no accounts yet, so the name is the key: keep the link and the groove is there when you come back (30 days after the last visit). Old `?room=` links redirect to `/sesh/<room>`.
- **Live line:** everything in the room edits one groove, and what your hands do is sent to everyone else the moment you do it: notes from the keys, the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard, pitch bend (at most 25 a second), stutter, play and stop. Notes another player plays sound here but are recorded only into their take, and when someone leaves, whatever they were holding is let go.
- **The maker's floor:** whoever opens a sesh first is its maker (a key kept in their browser, no account). Everyone plays in a freezone until the maker presses <kbd>Esc</kbd> or **Hold**: then only the maker edits and plays into the room, and everyone else hears it and plays along heard only by themselves. **Freezone** lets them back in. **Close out** ends the jam: everyone else gets a card (play on alone with the groove they had, or start a sesh), the groove is kept, and only the maker gets in, to **Reopen** it.
- **Grants, who plays, edits and mixes:** the maker leads the sesh. The people button in the status bar lists who's in, and the leader turns on each person's **Play** (the live line, takes and the studio's gear), **Edit** (the groove), **Mix** (mutes, solos, EQ, space and squash) and **Lead** (grant the others, hold the floor, set the code), or hands the lead on. Someone without a grant has the floor's (Freezone: play, edit and mix; Hold: listen) and raises a hand for one, which the leader answers Yes or No. The World checks every message against the grants, and they're kept beside the groove and in the backup. In Freezone a restriction only holds for people who keep their name: Hold is the one that locks.
- **Private seshes:** the maker clicks **Public** in the status bar and sets a room code (4 to 32 characters). From then on a joiner types the code first (the maker never does); this browser remembers it, and everyone already in gets a new code the moment it changes. **Make public** takes it off. The World keeps only a salted hash (restored from the backup with the groove), and an address that gets it wrong 5 times waits a minute.
- **Pointers:** you see where everyone else's mouse is, each in its own colour. Yours isn't drawn.
- **Yours alone:** **Save my setup** keeps a copy of the groove in this browser; **Back to my setup** puts it back after a kid or a parent has had a go (in a sesh, for everyone; undo brings theirs back). **Go solo** leaves the sesh for your own project, saved in this browser and never sent; **Back to the sesh** rejoins the shared groove. Accounts come later (`src/ui/mine.ts`).
- **Chat:** a chat box sits bottom right, with the sesh's last 300 lines kept beside the groove. Click the name at its top to pick yours; until you do, you show as `guest` plus four letters. The sesh's maker (the browser that opened it first, the one that can hold the floor) is its host and gets a delete on every line. A deleted line stays where it was, marked deleted, so the log doesn't jump. The log scrolls inside its box: drag the top edge to make it taller, widen it, or close it to a button that counts new lines.
- **You:** your browser keeps a random key, and the room and the profile store only ever see its hash. Your profile (your name, and whether the chat is open, wide and how tall) lives in a Profile Durable Object per person (`src/profile.ts`, `GET`/`PUT /me`), so it waits for you in every sesh. The page also keeps a copy in localStorage. There are no accounts yet; a sign-in can later point more than one key at one profile.
- **Limits:** `ROOM_SEATS` players in a room (16), `MAX_ROOMS` rooms open at once (50) and `MAX_SESHES` seshes kept at all (2,000, the storage cap), all set in `rustybuns.config.ts`. Past any of them, a visitor sees a card with a sign-up list, Try again, and Play alone (your solo project). The Lobby Durable Object (`src/lobby.ts`) does the counting. Read the list with `curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" <site>/signups`, where the token is the optional `SIGNUPS_TOKEN` secret in 1Password; without it, nobody can read the list.
- **Free live sets:** a listen link on a free account is rate-limited. A set ends at `LISTEN_SET_HOURS` (4), takes `LISTEN_EARS` listeners at once (16), and a DJ key plays one live set at a time across all its links. Each DJ key and each address get `FREE_SET_HOURS` set-hours a month (16, counted by calendar month in UTC). A set that is live counts at its full planned length against its address until it ends, so several DJ keys there can't share the same hours twice. A DJ who hits a limit sees a card on the decks naming it, with "Paid is coming: join the waitlist" (an email). A listener turned away from a full set gets the same card. The Lobby holds the live DJ keys (on a lease, so a crashed set frees itself after its planned end), counts the hours, and keeps the waitlist beside the full list: `<site>/signups?list=paid`. When a set ends, the Lobby gets one usage record (`at`, `hours`, `peakEars`, `chunks`, `endedBy` dj or limit). `curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" <site>/usage` returns each month's running totals and the last 500 records, so the limits can be set from real averages.
- **Wind back:** **Wind back…** in the status bar plays the groove as it was 1 min, 5, 10, 20, 1 h, 4 h or 1 day ago, here only: your edits wait and the others' keep landing on the sesh's groove, which **Back to now** returns to. The maker (the leader) can **Bring everyone back here**: that's one edit, so undo takes it back. The World keeps the groove as each edited minute began, thinned so a snapshot always sits a little past each rung (`src/rewind.ts`, about 25 at most), and the ladder goes into the backup with the groove. A take that was off every track when the room last emptied comes back silent.
- **Backup:** every sesh is copied to the `fm-daw-seshes` R2 bucket when its room empties, and every 5 minutes during a jam. If a room's Durable Object comes back empty, the groove, its maker and the floor are restored from the bucket, so a closed sesh stays closed.
- The desktop app runs the same World in-process, with no Lobby and no bucket: one room, never turned away, saved to sqlite.

## Play

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Space</kbd> | play / stop |
| <kbd>A</kbd> to <kbd>'</kbd> | a keyboard (Ableton's layout): white keys on the home row, black keys above |
| <kbd>Shift</kbd> + key | accented note |
| <kbd>Z</kbd> / <kbd>X</kbd> | octave down / up |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | record (MIDI, or audio while the input is open on a track); with **Count-in** on, one bar of clicks first |
| <kbd>1</kbd> to <kbd>0</kbd> | pick a track (the first ten) |
| <kbd>⌘Z</kbd> / <kbd>⇧⌘Z</kbd> | undo / redo |
| <kbd>V</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> <kbd>N</kbd> <kbd>M</kbd> (hold) | stutter: repeat the 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32 the playhead is in, until you let go |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | panic: stop everything, silence every voice (in a sesh you made, also hold the floor) |

- **Add to a track** (the four icons over the grid, the word on hover): **Record audio** opens your input on the selected track (the browser asks for the mic the first time; if it says no, the bar says so), counts in, records one pass of the loop and stops by itself. **Record MIDI** records the notes you play onto it. While either runs, the bar shows what is being recorded and onto which track, and the red square stops it. After a pass the input stays open on that track (a chip with its name; its cross closes it), so its Amp hears it and the next take asks for nothing. **Import audio** puts a sound file (anything the browser decodes: wav, mp3, m4a, ogg, flac) on the selected track as a take, mixed to mono, from the bar the view starts on; it says when the file was cut to the 32 seconds a take holds, or when four tracks already have one. **Import MIDI** reads a .mid (formats 0 and 1) note for note, beats as the file has them, with no quantizing. One part lands on the selected track; a file with several lets you pick one or spread them down from it, offers to grow the loop when the notes run past it, and to take the file's tempo. It replaces the notes in the bars it covers, and one undo takes it all back. Drop either kind of file on a track's name to import onto that track.
- **No instrument:** **Plays** on a track has **No instrument** beside its FM voice and the plugins. The track keeps its notes: they show in the editors, record and import as normal, and go to MIDI out and to the others in a sesh, but make no sound here, live or in the bounce. Its take still plays. The track list says **midi** for it.
- **Song** loads a whole project. **Long Trail** is ten minutes (432 bars) of dark, symphonic, dubby liquid drum and bass at 172 in A minor, a ride through the parks at night, in nine sections: Dawn, Trailhead, Canyon, Starfield, Summit, Dub Valley, Switchbacks, Ridgeline, Last Light. The drums never leave for long (an octave down through most of it, back up for the drops); the violin is there nearly throughout, plucked in the quiet parts and the builds and bowed for its two themes; the trumpet throws Spanish turns into the dub delay; long horn chords bloom underneath; and the big moves are hi-cut and low-cut sweeps across whole sections, written as automation, with one-beat cuts before each drop. **Night Shift** is the same material as a 32-bar loop. Every sound is a patch in `src/themes.ts` and `src/drums.ts`, so the songs are under this repo's license and use no samples.
- **Analog voice:** any track's patch can switch from **FM** to **Analog**: two band-limited saws detuned apart (up to ±50 cents), a sine sub an octave down, and noise, all through a resonant low-pass with its own envelope; one free-running LFO per track (a 50-second sweep up to 20 Hz) on cutoff and pitch, with vibrato fading in over the first 0.3 s of each note; glide and drive. Its amp envelope and level are operator 1's. This is the voice for detuned-saw basses, womps, brass and bowed strings (the noise is the bow) that FM can't make. Rust has it too, bit for bit.
- **Filters for the analog voice:** **SVF** (the 12 dB low-pass above), **Ladder** (four zero-delay one-poles with the feedback solved each sample and a tanh on the input, after Zavalishin's TPT ladder: the fat 24 dB old-school bass filter that growls when pushed), **Vowel** (two formant band-passes; cutoff slides them through oo, oh, ah, eh, ee, so an LFO on cutoff makes the bass talk), and **Diode** (the diode ladder from Zavalishin's chapter on it: four coupled stages, each loaded by the next, solved together each sample; thinner and squelchier than the ladder, the **Acid line**'s filter). The diode ladder was checked against the book before it was tuned: it whistles at a feedback of 17, its peak lands on the cutoff, and it falls 24 dB an octave.
- **Supersaw:** the **Supersaw** knob swaps the two saws for seven, after Adam Szabo's analysis of the 90s original: his measured spread for each saw, his fitted detune curve on **Detune** (gentle for the first half, wide at the top) and his mix curve on the knob itself (the centre saw against the six around it). The big trance lead and pad (**Supersaw lead**, **Supersaw pad**). It costs seven cheap PolyBLEP saws a voice and no `sin`.
- **Wavefolder:** **Fold** sends the oscillators through a sine fold before the filter, the West Coast shaper: past full scale the wave turns back on itself, up to four times, so the sound gets brighter and stranger as the knob comes up (**Folded bass**).
- **Pulses and PWM:** **Shape** fades both oscillators from saws to pulses (a saw minus a shifted saw, so the PolyBLEP smoothing carries over), and **PWM** lets the LFO narrow the pulse down to 5%.
- **LFO:** sine, triangle, saw down or square; free in Hz or **locked to the tempo** (1 bar down to 1/32, with triplets). A locked LFO follows the transport, so a 1/4 wobble peaks on the beat wherever you pressed play; **Restart on note** starts the wobble with each note instead. On an FM patch the same LFO moves modulation depth, for FM growls.
- **Squash** (next to Space): an over-the-top multiband compressor on the master. Linkwitz-Riley crossovers at 120 Hz and 2.5 kHz split the mix into three bands that sum back flat; each band's loud parts come down (4:1) and its quiet ones come up (3:1, up to 16 dB), so tails, breaths and reverb crowd forward and everything gets dense. The highs and lows get a small lift on top. It's written from the textbook design, runs after either engine and before Space (so Rust/TS parity is untouched) and ends in its own limiter at −1 dBFS. 0 is off and costs nothing.
- **Shape** (the tab next to Pattern): the master EQ, up to eight bands drawn over the live spectrum. Double-click the curve for a band (a **low cut** near the bottom end, a **high cut** near the top, a **bell** in between), drag a dot to move it (up and down is gain on a bell or shelf, and resonance on a cut: drag a cut up for a higher Q, a harder corner with a bump on it), roll the wheel over it for Q, Alt-click to bypass it, hold a dot still for a moment to hear only its range (it follows a drag; let go for the whole mix again), double-click it or press Delete to take it out; the strip under the curve sets the selected band exactly. Types: low cut and high cut (3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 and on up to 96 dB/oct, every one -3 dB at its corner; from 12 up they are Butterworth stacks, so the passband stays flat, and **Reso** above 0.71 puts a bump at the corner; 3 and 9 are half orders, a chain of seven first-order shelves that stays within 0.06 dB of a true 3 dB/oct slope for eight octaves), low and high shelf, bell (a bump up or a dip down, Q is how narrow; matched to the analog shape, so a bell up at 15 kHz keeps its width instead of narrowing near Nyquist), **notch** (a keyhole that takes a range clean out: low Q sweeps out a wide range, high Q one ringing tone) and **band pass** (only that range gets through: sweep it to find a ring, then notch it). **Phase** draws what the bands do to the phase (a steep cut turns it most; a notch flips it at its centre). Drag or roll a dot while recording and the move is kept as automation on the master, like a Perform move on a track: it plays back in the loop and the bounce, the strip under the dots lists each lane (band and Hz, gain or Q) with a clear button, and taking a band out takes its moves with it. **Shape on** (the picker at the left of its bar) lists every channel: the master, each track, each player the live line plays, the Space return and the aux 2 return. Each has its own eight bands, saved with the groove and heard live and in the bounce: a track's is filtered inside the engine before the tracks are summed (so its Space send hears the shaped sound too), Space's plays on its wet signal before its limiter, and aux 2's on what the cabled gear hands back. In the picker, a channel that has a Shape shows a dot after its name. The **Shape** button on every strip of the Mixer tab opens the same editor over the strips. Every Shape draws its own channel's live spectrum while it is open (a track's, a player's, a return's, here and on the Mixer tab), copied out of the mix as it plays, so the mix is unchanged. Only the master's Shape listens to one band and automates.
  - *How it's built:* every band is a trapezoidal state-variable filter (Andrew Simper's linear trap SVF), which stays stable and click-free while you sweep it, unlike a direct-form biquad. Bands run in series, so their phase shifts add but never cancel into ripples, and their order doesn't change the sound. It's minimum phase with no latency; frequencies are prewarped, so a corner lands where it's drawn, and the curve is the filters' exact digital response (bells near Nyquist narrow a little, and the curve shows that too). Settings glide over 20 ms, switching a band crossfades it, and it ends in its own limiter at −1 dBFS. It's written in Rust (`native/crates/fm_daw/src/eq.rs`, in the same wasm as the engine) and again in TypeScript (`src/engine/eq.ts`): the worklet runs the Rust one with the Rust engine and the TypeScript one with the TS engine, and they match bit for bit. It sits after the engine and before Squash, in the audio thread and the bounce, and is saved with the project (and shared in a sesh).
- **Chorus, flanger or phaser** on every track (**Mod**): the 80s polysynth bucket-brigade chorus, built from the circuit's published figures. One delay line swept by a triangle (I slow, II faster, I+II a fast shallow shimmer), darkened, added on the left and subtracted on the right. **Flange** sweeps the same line shorter (0.3 to 4 ms) and feeds it back, the jet (**Jet pad**). **Phase** sweeps four allpasses from 150 Hz to 3 kHz with feedback, two moving notches (**Phased keys**).
- **Sounds:** **Load a sound** on any track: wobble bass, talking growl, Reese, FM growl, ladder bass, acid line, folded bass, sub, screech, supersaw lead, ladder lead, chorus pad, supersaw pad, PWM strings, poly brass, chorus keys, jet pad, phased keys, minimal stab, blip, dub chord (`src/sounds.ts`). The track keeps its pan.
- **Clips** (under the drum grid or piano roll, on a track with recorded audio, #5578): it follows the zoom, under the same ruler and playhead. Each clip draws its sound, bright where it plays and dim where it is there but not on. Drag an edge to trim it (Shift snaps to the Grid), drag the middle to move it on the Grid, **Alt-drag** (or the Slip tool) slides the sound under a window that stays put, to line a hit up with the beat; the **Cut** tool or **C** over the lane splits a clip on the Grid, **Delete** takes the picked one off. A cut shares its sound (stored once; `MAX_TAKES` counts sounds), and a trimmed or cut edge fades over 3 ms so it never clicks. A track nobody cut keeps its old `take`; `clipsOf()` in `src/take.ts` reads either form. `fmdaw.land(track, beat, pcm, rate)` lays audio on a track from a script, and `fmdaw.state().tracks[i].clips` reads them back.
- **Perform** (under the grid, on the selected track): hold a **stutter**; switch the **wobble rate** on the beat; drag the **pad** (cutoff across, wobble depth up); **bend** up to an octave either way, springing back on release. A MIDI keyboard's pitch wheel bends ±2. **MIDI learn**: turn it on, touch any slider, move a knob or fader on your controller, and that CC drives it on whichever track is selected (log sliders keep their travel). Out of the box the mod wheel is wobble depth and CC 74 is cutoff; the map is kept in this browser, since it belongs to your controller rather than the song. A stutter repeats its slice while the song runs on underneath, so letting go lands where the song would have been. **Record** (R) and your stutter, bend and pad moves become automation on the selected track, so the take plays back the way you played it; the list under the bend shows the track's lanes, each with a button to clear it (undo brings it back). Outside a take, stutter and bend aren't saved. Both engines hear them alike, since the stutter and the lanes live in the sequencer.
- **Wobble Lab** (Song menu): half-time at 140 in F minor, four bars to play over. The wobble's rate is automated so it talks, and a vowel growl answers in bar 4.
- **Vel tone** (every patch): how much harder hits change the sound, not just its level. Analog: the cutoff moves up to 3 octaves either way. FM: modulation depth and feedback scale from nothing to double.
- **Duck under** (every patch, in Mix): a sidechain. Pick another track (the kick) and this one dips each time that one plays and comes back over 150 ms, so the kick pumps the bass or the pad. **Duck** is how far: 60% when you first pick a track, 100% down to silence on the loudest hits. Both engines do it the same way, sample for sample, live and in the bounce; it dips the track's sends with it. A new sound or Randomize keeps it. A track's recorded clips, a plugin track (its Mix has **Duck under** too) and a player playing into the track duck with it, live and in the bounce. They can't make another track duck yet (#6215).
- **Hi-cut and low-cut** on every track: a low-pass closing from 20 kHz and a high-pass rising from 20 Hz, after the track's voices. At 0 they're off. Set them to carve room for one part, or automate them for long sweeps over a section.
- **Automation:** a track can carry lanes (`auto` in the project) that move any parameter along straight lines between points; two points on one beat make a jump, which is how cuts and articulation changes (the violin going from plucked to bowed) are written. The sequencer applies them, so both engines hear the same thing. Songs write them today; there's no lane editor yet.
- **Long songs:** a project can run up to 512 bars. The step grid and piano roll show any stretch of it you like (`src/ui/view.ts`, `src/ui/Timeline.tsx`): the overview bar above them is the whole song small with your view as a box (drag it to slide, drag its edges or pinch to zoom, double-click to fit), zoom buttons and keys sit beside it, the ruler counts bars or beats by how far in you are, and the grid thins as you zoom out and comes down to the Grid choice as you zoom in. A note that starts before the view but plays into it shows clipped at the left edge, and an edit never moves a note outside the view. Follow the playhead (the target button) slides the view along as it plays. `fmdaw.view(from, span)` and `fmdaw.state().view` for probes. Bounce renders a song (anything past 32 bars) once instead of four times.
- **Drum machine** (above the step grid): **Groove** writes a pattern onto the five drum tracks and moves BPM and swing to it (liquid roller, late night, half-time, chopped break, steppers, four on the floor). Loops run 1 to 32 bars. Loops of 2 bars or more get the groove's fill in the last bar. **Kit** swaps the drum patches and keeps the pattern. Each is one undo.
- **Space** is a reverb and a dotted-eighth ping-pong delay fed by each track's **Send** (lightly high-passed at 150 Hz), so the kick and bass can stay dry and mono while stabs and leads drown. The tails go wide and drift slowly across the stereo field. Higher Space also means more delay feedback; a saturator in the loop keeps the repeats from running away, and a slow wobble on the delay time bends them a little. The engine renders the send bus next to the mix (Rust and TS match on it too); Space runs after either engine, in the audio thread and in the bounce, and has its own limiter at −1 dBFS.

Each deck shows its BPM and a beat grid. Each deck is a club media player: the screen (the overview: paused, click it to put the track there or drag across it to scrub; while the deck plays a press on it does nothing, so a stray click mid-set can't throw the track), a big jog wheel, a big **CUE** (stopped: mark and play while held, back to the mark on letting go; playing: back to the mark and stop; press play while holding it and it plays on) and a big play/pause. The jog's **VINYL** / **CD** switch (VINYL to start): in VINYL, hold the top platter and the track stops under your hand and drag it to scratch, forwards and back, and the side ring nudges; in CD the whole wheel nudges. Stopped, a turn steps through the track. Drag it, touch it, scroll on it, or press **MIDI** (the round socket button, its count the controllers plugged in; one press connects them all, and any plugged in later join by themselves) for a controller. Its jog works out of the box (MIDI channel 1 deck A, 2 deck B; note 54 the platter's touch, CC 33 the ring and CC 34 the platter, relative). The cable button beside it shows the connections (`src/dj/midi.ts`): a see-through tag on each control a controller drives, its name and message ("DDJ-400 · CC 19 ch2"), floating over the decks and the mixer so nothing moves; an unplugged controller's tags show it gone. While they show, click any knob, fader, button or jog, then move a control on the controller: that pairs them, kept in this browser by the controller's name, and a tag's x forgets one (`qdecks.midi`). Then **SYNC** (the other deck's tempo, and its beat when both play), the tempo fader with a button for each range (±6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 25, 50 or 100%; down is faster), **MT** (master tempo: the key stays put as the tempo moves, a time-stretch in the deck's own worklet, `src/dj/stretch.ts`; off, tempo and pitch move together like a record), and the **START** and **BRAKE** knobs (how long play takes to come up to speed and pause to stop, up to 2 and 4 s, the pitch rising and falling with it like a turntable's motor and brake; at 0 they are instant, and a cue or a scratch is always instant). Four hot cues (shift-click clears one), loops of ½ to 16 beats on the grid, **IN** / **OUT** / **RELOOP** (IN marks a loop's start, OUT its end and it loops; press OUT again while it loops and the jog moves the out point, clockwise longer and back shorter, the wheel's screen saying LOOP OUT, and OUT once more is done; IN again does the same for the in point; RELOOP plays on out of the loop, or back into the last one), and 4-beat jumps. Both close-up waveforms run across the top on one time scale with their beat grids, so the kicks line up when they're matched; its **+** and **−** (or a pinch) zoom from a quarter second to sixteen either side, drawn from peaks kept at a few resolutions so it stays smooth. The mixer (`src/dj/mixer.ts`, its face `mixer-panel.ts`) is laid out like a four-channel analog club mixer with twin filters: deck A plays into channel 2 and B into 3, and channels 1 and 4 wait for more inputs. The mixer on the DJ table in 3D (`mixer-club-4` in `@rustybuns/models` gear.ts) is this same face, every control where the page has it: the filter columns either side of the channels, the black master panel at the right, the crossfader bed under the four, slate with white print and the lit keys orange; its channel meters light while their deck plays. Each channel, top to bottom as on the hardware: two aux sends, trim, a four-band EQ (high and low cut to a full kill, the two mids to -26 dB, all boost +6), a flip switch for the filter (1, OFF or 2) and one for the crossfader (X, OFF for THRU, or Y), a round CUE button, then a dot LED meter beside a long fader. The two filters are full-height columns either side of the channels, the LFO at the top and the filter under it: on/off, high-, band- and low-pass (press more than one: high + low is a notch), a big sweep knob from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, resonance from mild to wild, and an LFO each (LFO 1 and LFO 2) that swings the sweep: depth, ½ to 16 beats a round, a shape (sine, triangle, square, ramp up, ramp down), and its tempo from **DECK A** (filter 1 to start), **DECK B** (filter 2) or **TAP**; on a deck it keeps to that deck's beat grid, so each round starts on a downbeat, and a lamp beside it pulses with it. The curve is drawn above. Each filter's output goes through a soft clip, as an analog filter's does, so a wild resonance sings rather than slamming the master's limiter. The face is slate metal with white print, black knurled knobs (the EQ with a dashed scale round each), round rubber buttons whose rings light orange, LEDs green, blue from 0 dB and red, and the master and monitor section on a black panel at the right; under 470 px the filters sit side by side under the channels; **NIGHT** turns the face down and the glow up, and a knob you hover shows its value (dB, Hz, Q, seconds) in its label. Then the crossfader in its sunken bed under the channels (smooth or cut), the master into a limiter with stereo meters, the booth level (your own speakers, apart from what the room's PA hears), the headphones' cue mix and level, and **Split cue** (one output: the cue in your left ear, the master in your right). Knobs turn by a drag up or down (Shift for fine), the wheel or the arrow keys; a double-click resets one. With **Quantize** on, cues and loops land on the beat. The close-up's waveforms are coloured by their sound, as a club player's are, so a kick and a clap look different: deck A is fire, deep red to near white, on the top lane, and deck B runs from blue-green (the bass, the kick) to a whitish purple (claps, hats) under it (`toneOf` in `src/dj/player.ts`). Press on a deck's lane of the close-up and you have its track under your hand like a record: drag to scratch, let go and it plays on (or **Slide** in Settings: the drag moves it and it keeps playing). Each player has a nudge row: the single arrows nudge the track back or on by the nudge amount (10 ms to start), one press one move, so you tap them as often as you need; the double arrows are the 10x nudge, held: the deck drifts ten nudges a second either way until you let go. **Settings** (the cog, `src/dj/prefs.ts`, kept in this browser): the nudge amount (1 to 200 ms), the waveform's colours or plain, what a grab does, locks (the pitch faders, the jog wheels, the waveform grab, loading onto a playing deck), and every key: press an action, then its new key. Keys to start: Q and P play A and B, W and O cue, 1 to 4 and 7 to 0 hot cues, A/S and K/L nudge, Z/X and comma/period the 10x nudge held, the arrows slide the crossfader. `qdecks` on /decks.html for probes.

- **Who may spin: a lineup or open decks:** each room has decks rules of its own on top of the grants (`src/spin.ts`, one guard per shape that the World and the page both import). `Spin` is `{ mode: "lineup" | "open", mods, refused, lineup: Slot[], on?, queue }`, and a `Slot` is `{ who, at, min }`: a person, a start on the room's clock and a length in minutes. **Lineup:** the decks are whoever's slot it is now. Play / stop, the room's master level, and a fader and a mute for each of the twelve tracks, named and coloured as on the desk. The track at the seat you sit at in the jam room (as the same person: signed in, or in the same browser) comes up top with its volume, a tone knob (the track's hi-cut) and a space knob (its Space send); sitting nowhere, tap a track's name. It sends the desk's own ops, so the leader's grants hold: Mix for the faders and knobs, Play for the transport. The room's level (`master` in the groove) sits on top of each listener's own master on the desk and in the jam room, and a bounce leaves it out (`src/remote/`). `qremote.state()` for probes.
- **Takes, audio on a track:** **Record audio** (over the grid) opens your audio input (a guitar, a mic, or a desk's out through your interface) with no echo cancelling, gating or levelling, onto the selected track, and records one pass of the loop from wherever you are, starting at the beat you heard (the output's and input's latency are taken off); a pass cut short by Stop doesn't replace a whole one. The take plays on its track after the engine, at its beat, wrapping round the loop, and mutes and solos with it; the track shows a waveform mark and **Clear take** takes it off (undo puts it back). In a sesh the take's sound goes to the room as 16-bit chunks at 24 kHz before the edit that places it, so everyone hears the same thing, and whoever joins later gets it after the groove. Takes are in the bounce, mixed in as they play. Online, the World copies each whole take to the backup bucket once, so a sesh whose Durable Object is reset gets its takes back with the groove; a solo project keeps its takes' sound in this browser (IndexedDB). Up to four tracks hold a take, 32 seconds each (`src/take.ts`). This is the gear room's step 6: once the amp is a track insert and the desk exists, their output feeds the same recorder.
- **Drums** show as a step grid: click toggles, shift-click accents. Notes you play in freely land between steps: the step shows striped with a tick where each hit sits, and an accent leaves it there. The grid's own Snap magnet (off to start) makes a click put a free hit on its step first; the next click clears it.
- **Synths** show as a piano roll with four tools. The pencil (B, the default) draws: drag on empty space, you hear it as you press, the start lands on the Grid and the end goes exactly where you let go. The eraser (E) takes off each note you click or drag across (right click, or Delete on a focused note, does too). The scissors (C) open a note's popover, a third of the window wide, with a thumb for its start and one for its end: drag, touch-drag, scroll or arrow either one, the start may go earlier than it was, and the whole popover is one undo step (Esc or a click outside closes it). The hand (H) carries a note in time and pitch with no snapping. On a focused note arrows move it and Shift and arrows stretch it. Snap (N, the magnet) puts the pencil's start and the thumbs on the Grid choice; with Snap off, or Alt held, they go exactly where the pointer is, since a note's start and length are any number of beats (`src/roll.ts`). Every edit is also a `desk_` command (`desk_roll_tool`, `desk_roll_notes`, `desk_draw_note`, `desk_erase_note`, `desk_set_note_ends`, `desk_move_note`) for WebMCP, `window.__commands` and the palette.
- **Quantize** a track to 1/4 through 1/32 or triplets, at any strength. 50% keeps half the feel. The magnet by Record MIDI (**Quantize input**) does it as you record; it starts off, so what you play lands where you played it.
- **MIDI keyboards** work as soon as they're plugged in (Web MIDI, no driver). On drum tracks middle C plays the drum's own note. MIDI "all notes off" triggers the panic.
- **Your own plugins (VST, AU, CLAP):** **To my DAW** under the keys sends every note played on the desk, yours and everyone's on the live line, to a MIDI port: track 1 on channel 1, track 12 on channel 12. On macOS turn on the IAC Driver in Audio MIDI Setup (Windows: a loopMIDI port), then in your DAW give an instrument track that port and channel as its input. This way the page loads no plugin; your DAW already hosts them. Panic lets go of all sixteen channels. Only played notes go out for now, not the sequencer's.
- **Web plugins (Web Audio Modules), in the browser too:** **Plays** on a track's sound panel also lists **Test Tone (web)** and **A web plugin from a link…**. Paste the address of a [Web Audio Module](https://www.webaudiomodules.com/) (WAM 2, an `index.js` on https) and it plays the track's notes in the page: its parameters are the track's sliders (MIDI learn works), **Editor** opens its own panel, and its sound goes through the track's fader and mute into the master's Shape and Squash. A plugin is code, so one from another site stays silent and asks first: **Let <site> run plugins here** is each player's own yes, kept on that computer, and a friend's groove can't answer it for you. Say yes only for a site you know: its code can do anything the page can, as you. **Insert**, next to **Plays** on an FM track, puts a web effect on the track's sound (#5359): **Gain (web)** is FM DAW's own, or **A web effect from a link…**, with the same per-site yes. The track's sound leaves the engine on a channel of its own, goes through the module and comes back into the mix a render quantum (under 3 ms) late; it plays dry until the effect runs, or when one won't load. A module that says it's an instrument is refused there, and one that says it's an effect is refused under **Plays**. The return follows the track's **PAN**, and its **SEND** feeds Space after the effect (#5551). A web plugin track has an **Insert** too: its sound goes through the effect after its fader. An amped track keeps its amp, and its Insert gets silence. A bounce has the effect in it: the track is rendered on its own, then played through a copy of the effect offline. On a web plugin track the bounce's effect comes before the fader. An effect that won't play is named in the bounce's note, and its track goes in dry. Try it with real sound in headless Chrome: `bun scripts/web-plugin-check.ts`.
- **Bounce** renders four loops to a WAV in `~/Music/FM DAW` (set `FM_DAW_DIR` to change it), and **Show** opens it in Finder. Online, the sesh also keeps its last bounce in the bucket (`GET /sesh/<name>/bounce`, the tempo in `X-Bpm`; a private sesh's needs `?code=`), so a DJ booth's deck in Sound Guy Simulator can play it by the sesh's name. It is deleted with the sesh.

## Where the new voices come from

We looked for open-source Rust synth code to borrow, and checked each license. The engine is written twice (TS and Rust, bit for bit), so we port algorithms rather than add crates. The ladder, diode ladder, vowel filter, pulses, chorus, flanger, phaser, supersaw, wavefolder and Squash here are written from the published designs: Zavalishin's *The Art of VA Filter Design* for the ladder, the diode ladder and SVFs, the textbook flanger (a swept delay with feedback) and phaser (swept first-order allpasses), the Peterson–Barney vowel formants, published measurements of the bucket-brigade chorus, Adam Szabo's *How to Emulate the Super Saw* (2010) for the supersaw's offsets and curves, the textbook sine folder, and Linkwitz-Riley crossovers with up/down compression for Squash. No code was copied, so there are no notices to carry. Good places to look next:

| Project | License | Worth porting |
|---|---|---|
| [fundsp](https://github.com/SamiPerttu/fundsp) | Apache-2.0 (ours) | comb, wavefolding shapers (our phaser and flanger are written from the textbook, not ported) |
| [valib](https://github.com/SolarLiner/valib) | MIT | transistor and OTA ladder variants, saturators, oversampling |
| [Mutable Instruments eurorack](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack) | MIT | PWM and variable-shape oscillators, wavefolder, formant speech, swarm (supersaw-like) |

| Surge XT, Vital, Odin 2, HexoDSP/synfx-dsp, OctaSine, Dexed | GPL/AGPL | read the papers they cite instead; don't port the code |

## Plugins: what they cost, and whose licence (#4899)

Checked 2026-11-19, before building more on them. Short answer: hosting plugins costs nothing and needs no agreement, as long as FM DAW only loads what a player already has.

| Format | Licence of the format | Fee or agreement | What we do |
|---|---|---|---|
| [CLAP](https://github.com/free-audio/clap) | MIT | none: no fee, no membership, no sign-up ([cleveraudio.org](https://cleveraudio.org/1-feature-overview/_open_licnese/)) | in use on the desktop. The host is written by hand from the published headers (`native/crates/fm_daw/src/clap.rs`), with no crate |
| [VST 3](https://steinbergmedia.github.io/vst3_dev_portal/pages/VST+3+Licensing/Index.html) | MIT since SDK 3.8 (2025-10-29); before that GPLv3 or a signed agreement with Steinberg | none now | in use on the desktop (#5268), behind the same surface as CLAP: written by hand from the SDK 3.8 headers (`native/crates/fm_daw/src/vst3.rs`), with no crate; its editor opens in a window of ours (#5516). Use SDK 3.8 or later, or a binding made from it; an older binding may still be GPLv3, which would spread to the app. "VST" is a Steinberg trademark: the MIT licence doesn't cover it, so plain text "VST3" to say what loads is fine under their [usage guidelines](https://steinbergmedia.github.io/vst3_dev_portal/pages/VST+3+Licensing/Usage+guidelines.html), and the logo needs those guidelines followed |
| Audio Units | Apple's, part of macOS | none to host | in use on macOS (#5517), behind the same surface: written by hand over AudioToolbox (`native/crates/fm_daw/src/au.rs`), with no crate; editors aren't opened yet |
| [Web Audio Modules](https://github.com/webaudiomodules) | MIT | none | the browser's host (#5359, #5360 for what's left) |

- **The plugins themselves** are each under their maker's licence, and many are paid. FM DAW never ships, uploads or copies one: it loads the file from the player's own plugin folders, and a groove holds only the plugin's id, name and slider values. Keep it that way; a "share this plugin with the sesh" button would be handing out someone's product.
- **The stand-in** (a friend without the plugin hears the player's track on the live line) sends sound, not the plugin. That is what a player does when they stream or record a song, and instrument and effect licences ordinarily allow that (we read no single plugin's licence; a player's own plugin is theirs to check). Sample libraries sometimes forbid passing on single raw samples; a mix through the sesh isn't that, but don't build a feature that records a plugin note by note into a sampler.
- **Money:** nothing to pay anyone. The stand-in costs what a guitar on the live line does (Opus at 96 kb/s through the World, no new service).
- **Not checked:** AAX (needs a signed agreement with its owner and their signing tool; out of scope), and each store's rules if the desktop app is ever sold through one.

## Speaker safety

FM goes from pleasant to screaming in one slider move, so there are limits on every path to the speaker:

- Audio starts only after you click through a "speakers down first" screen, with the master at −12 dB.
- Every parameter is clamped to a range in the engine itself (`RANGES` in `src/engine/params.ts`, mirrored in Rust), so no slider, preset, MIDI message or bad network op can push a value past what's been tested. Modulation depth and operator feedback are capped below where FM turns to noise.
- The master chain blocks DC, smooths the master gain, resets the engine and outputs silence if a sample is ever NaN, then runs a peak limiter (instant attack) with a hard clamp at **−1 dBFS**. The **Limit** lamp lights when it's working.
- Every track starts at gain 0, so a fresh engine is silent until a project loads.
- `test/engine.test.ts` checks all of this: every param at its maximum with every voice on, NaN and Infinity everywhere, and 200 random patches. All of them stay finite and under the ceiling.

## Testing

`bun run bench [seconds]` races the engines in the terminal on each built-in song: Rust over bun:ffi (after `build:native`) and TypeScript under Bun, the same host lanes as the app's engine race.

`bun test test` runs the safety and parity tests. Look for `max |rust - ts| = 0.00e+0` on both the `cdylib` and `wasm` lines; without `build:native` those two skip with a note.

`bun run test:rust` builds the wasm and runs the Rust-backed tests: the circuit amps (`test/circuit.test.ts`) and the speaker boxes (`test/enclosure.test.ts`: the Rust box's curve and audio against TypeScript's on every plan, then a walk through `/speakers.html` at desktop and phone size). The page's handle is `window.qspeakers`: `state()` (plan, material, braces, explode, engine `rust` | `ts`, air in L, tune, q, down3, down6, portSpeed, dB at 30, 50, 100 and 1000 Hz, walls with hz, db and ringMs, the cabinet's parts, the cut list, frontZ), `pick(planId)`, `material(id)`, `braces(n)`, `explode(0..1)`, `render('box' | 'flat' | 'knock')` (peak and rms, no sound), `await listen(kind)`, `stop()`, `await glb()` and `parity()` (the largest Rust to TypeScript gap, or null with no wasm). `bun run probe fm-daw /speakers.html --js "qspeakers.state()"` reads it.

Then check by ear, **speakers down first**:

1. **Sound.** Space plays the demo; the **Limit** lamp should stay mostly dark. Switch the engine to Rust while it plays: nothing should change except the notes sounding at that moment. Sweep the Kick's pitch drop, depth and feedback, and hit Randomize on a few tracks, listening for anything harsh or clicky. Esc silences everything.
2. **Recording.** Press <kbd>7</kbd> (Keys), turn on Count-in, press <kbd>R</kbd>, play along after four clicks; notes appear in the roll. Record a drum (Quantize input, the magnet, starts off): hits show striped until you quantize them (try 50%). <kbd>⌘Z</kbd> undoes.
3. **MIDI.** Plug in a keyboard: its name appears by the keys without a reload. Play a synth track and a drum track.
4. **Saving.** Change something, quit, run again: it's still there. Bounce, then Show: the WAV sounds like what you heard.
5. **The binary.** `bun run desktop:build`, run `dist/fm-daw-<os>-<arch>`, repeat the above. Engine race should list all four lanes.

`rm -rf ~/.fm-daw` starts over from the demo groove. Not covered yet: Linux and Windows builds.

## Rust vs TypeScript

The golden tests render 8 seconds of the demo groove with each engine, and 8 seconds of Night Shift (the analog voice) over FFI. Both the cdylib and the wasm build match TypeScript **bit for bit** (max difference 0 over 384,000 frames), so switching engines changes nothing you can hear.

**Engine race** renders 20 s of the current groove on each lane. On an M-series Mac: TypeScript in the window about 15× realtime, Rust as wasm about 18×, Rust over FFI on the host about 18 to 21×, TypeScript under Bun about 16 to 19×. Rust's lead is small here because both engines spend their time in the same `sin` and `pow` calls. The interesting part is that the same audio comes out of four runtimes.

## On Cloudflare

`src/worker.ts` serves the page and routes `/ws?room=<name>` to a `World` Durable Object, the same class the desktop runs. Open the same room in two browsers and edits show up in both; the status bar counts who's jamming. Visitors are anonymous: the Worker vouches a random id. One address can open only so many new rooms a minute (per Worker isolate), a room holds `ROOM_SEATS` sockets (16), and a socket sending more than its budget (120 messages a second, big ones cost more) is closed and reconnects to the snapshot. A room nobody opens for 30 days is deleted; the desktop's own groove never is. The page is a plain SPA: the Rust engine runs as wasm in the browser's AudioWorklet, exactly as in the desktop window; only the host's extras (saving bounces to `~/Music`, Show, the Engine race's host lanes) stay desktop-only, and Bounce downloads the WAV instead.

Deploying is yours to run, from this folder: `bunx rustybuns plan` (creates nothing; it lists `[Build]`, `[Worker]` and `[Worker/WORLD]`), then `bunx rustybuns deploy`. Both need a Cloudflare login (`bunx rustybuns login cloudflare`). The deploy's build step is `bun run build`, so the wasm ships when cargo and `wasm32-unknown-unknown` are installed. Send a friend your `https://<the deployed url>/sesh/<name>`: everyone with that link edits one groove. A bare `/` makes a new sesh.

## Known limits

- A YouTube deck's sound stays inside YouTube's player, so the EQ, filters, loops, sync and scratch don't reach it and it has no waveform or beat grid. The room hears it through each listener's own player, not the PA: no room ring, no delay from the speakers, and within about a third of a second of the DJ's.
- Master tempo is a plain time-stretch (WSOLA), checked only by numbers (a tone keeps its pitch and level at 0.9, 1.08 and 1.5): how it sounds on real tracks at wide ranges needs ears, and below a quarter speed the deck falls back to plain pitch. There is no saved library (tracks are loaded each visit). MIDI learn is checked only with messages fed to the page (`qdecks.midi.feed`), not yet with a real controller in hand. In the booth a deck plays from the machine that loaded it, so it goes when its DJ leaves, and back to back you hear your own deck at once and theirs a PA call later (about 100 to 200 ms); the pitch fader and loops of someone else's deck are theirs alone, and a scratch on it from your wheel is heard a round trip late, and each DJ's mixer shapes only the decks they play.
- **The sound has only been checked by numbers**, not by ear: levels, peaks and parity are tested, and a bounce of the demo peaks at −3.8 dBFS. How the kit sounds needs your ears.
- **Switching engines cuts the notes that are sounding**; the groove carries on from the next note.
- **Event timing is quantized to 16 samples** (a third of a millisecond at 48 kHz) to keep the audio thread simple.
- **Edits go to everyone, last write wins.** There's no conflict merging beyond that, and undo is per person.
- **No MIDI clock yet, and MIDI out sends played notes only.** MIDI learn maps sliders only, not the button rows (filter type, LFO shape, chorus).
- **No input monitoring:** you don't hear the input through the app while recording (use your interface's monitoring).
- **Voice stealing can click** when a track plays more than 8 notes at once.
