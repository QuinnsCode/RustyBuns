# Git Recon

> **What it proves:** a repo can be a level. Real ground, a codebase laid onto it, and a third-person shooter played through it, in the page on your own, or by up to 64 players in one battle that the desktop app hosts in its world and opens to the LAN, or online in a Durable Object per room. One of the [Rusty Buns](../../README.md#see-it-work) examples; all of them are in [EXAMPLES.md](../../EXAMPLES.md).

A toy-soldier third-person shooter, fought in a codebase. You're a glossy black clay soldier with a rifle: pick a thick, average or lank build, and swap your head for a logo. The repo's biggest folders are compounds in conflict, held by orange guards; its files are the buildings you fight around, and the bigger the file, the taller the building. What it is decides its shape: code is a tower, a doc a house with a pitched roof, config a silo, a test a bunker under a slab, styles and assets a stepped stack. Down a compound's guards and the folder is merged. Merge them all and you win.

The story: somebody committed the nuclear launch codes to main, and the Conflict's folders want them. The campaign plays it straight, through hedgerows, farmhouses, shelled villages and a landing craft, with command on the radio sounding like a techno-thriller read by someone who hasn't slept.

- **Real ground or toy ground.** *El Capitan*, *Half Dome* and *Glacier Point* are Yosemite from Park Hide & Seek's elevation data, shrunk eight times. *The Backyard*, *The Kitchen Floor* (table and chair legs, a sink cabinet too sheer to climb) and *The Garden* (vegetable rows, a hose, a pond to wade) are authored the way Druids Curse builds terrain: rolling noise plus stamps.
- **Anywhere is a level.** Type a latitude, longitude on the menu and the page reads the ground there from AWS Terrain Tiles, decodes it itself, and keeps it in the browser for next time. Pick *Anywhere* as a battle's level to host one there: the ground goes out with the setup, so nobody else has to fetch it.
- **The weapons are git.** The weapon list in the corner draws each one beside its command (no "git", since they all are). `git commit` is the rifle (always another magazine), `git add -A` the shotgun (ten pellets, full damage inside six metres and nothing past fifteen: one blast ends a soldier up close), `git fetch --all` the machine gun (hold it and it climbs off target; let go and it settles), `git clean -fdx` the flamethrower (ten metres of fire that sets guards running about alight; dive-roll or wade in to put yourself out), `git push` the bazooka (splash and a shove), `git merge` the grenade (lobbed, bounces), `git rebase -i` the sniper (a near-instant round, five to a magazine, one to the body downs a guard, two a shielded soldier, and a headshot drops even a heavy), `git cherry-pick` a round that homes on the guard nearest your aim, `git revert` sends a guard back where it was three seconds ago and makes it forget you, `git stash` throws down a box to hide behind that the guards shoot instead of you, `git blame` tags a guard so you see it through walls. `git reset --hard` is a rare crate: wherever it lands, that yard's guards all go down.
- **The scope steps in, like a console shooter's.** With the sniper out, right click (or the scope button on touch) steps the scope in to 3.5×, again to 9×, again back out; you don't hold it. The reticle is a hairline cross with range ticks, the magnification above and the range to what's under it below, and it drifts with the sight's sway (steadier crouched, steadiest prone). The mouse slows with the view, so a degree of screen is a degree of screen at any magnification. A hit knocks you out of the scope, and so does a dive-roll or a change of gun; fired from the hip the rifle is a guess.
- **Magazines, not spam.** Every gun fires from a magazine and has to be reloaded (`R`, or it starts by itself when one runs dry): thirty rounds for `git commit`, six shells for `git add -A`, one rocket for `git push`. The crosshair rings round while a fresh one goes in, and the soldier dips the gun and reaches for it.
- **A pistol and plasma.** Every kit ends in `git commit --amend`, a slow accurate pistol whose spare magazines never run out (so a dry role always has a last shot; two to the head from a distance). `git merge --squash` is the plasma grenade: lobbed, it sticks to whatever it lands on, a soldier included, and goes off 2.4 seconds after it was thrown. Committer, Maintainer and Releaser carry plasma; Reviewer is the sniper.
- **Roles.** You carry a role's kit, not everything: *Committer* (rifle, shotgun, grenades, a stash), *Maintainer* (machine gun, flamethrower, bazooka), *Reviewer* (sniper and shotgun, the "shotty snipes" kit, plus homing rounds, revert, blame) or *Releaser* (shotgun, bazooka, grenades, fire). The first gun's spare magazines never run out; the rest come with what they come with, plus crates. Pick one on the menu or the results screen for the next round, or press `N` mid-fight to change it for your next life. In a battle everyone carries every gun.
- **The ground is the code.** It's printed with the repo's own source: the opening lines of its biggest files, each under an orange header with its path, in columns like a newspaper page, fading out on rock faces. A loaded GitHub repo prints its own.
- **Hills are hard work.** The steeper the slope, the slower you climb it (and a little quicker coming down); too steep to stand on and you slide back to the bottom. Guards slog up the same hills.
- **Things blow up.** Each yard has orange barrels of build artifacts that explode when shot and set off the ones beside them, a beat apart. A file that comes down goes up with a bang, bigger the bigger the file.
- **Killstreaks.** On your own, down 15 guards in a level and a rocket roadster drops in beside you; 25 and it's an attack helicopter. `F` gets in and out. The roadster runs guards over, smashes files it hits at speed and has a gun on the rear deck that fires where you aim. Hold `Space` and its thrusters lift it off the ground and push it whichever way `W`/`A`/`S`/`D` say, over files and cliffs, until they run dry; they fill again once it's back on its wheels. The helicopter flies the way you look (`Space` climbs, `C` drops), with a chin gun on click and rockets on right click. Both take the hits meant for you, and when one is wrecked it blows up and throws you clear.
- **The files come down.** Every building is a file, and blasts, fire and bullets delete its lines: it sinks as it loses them, the crosshair reads how many are left, and one with none left bursts into paper chips and is gone. Cover wears away under fire, yours and theirs. The flamethrower burns a file down in a second.
- **Guns with weight.** Recoil kicks your aim up (less crouched, least prone) and the gun up in the soldier's hands, blasts shake the view, bullets draw as tracers, and the crosshair flashes on a hit, harder on a kill, and an orange double ring on a headshot. Every git command has its own gun in the hands: a tube for `git push`, a drum for `git fetch --all`, a tank for `git clean -fdx`, a scope for `git rebase -i`.
- **A shield over your health.** It soaks hits first (an orange shimmer round you, a fizz), pops when it runs out, and four seconds after the last hit a band sweeps up you as it refills. A heavy has one too. An orange arc round the crosshair points at whoever hit you.
- **Drawn like a comic.** Glow vanishes on white paper, so the action is inked: muzzle flashes are orange starbursts, blasts are an orange core under inked puffs of smoke with a ring running out along the ground, and each one throws a quick orange light on what's near it. The soldiers carry an ink outline, and the shadows round you stay crisp.
- **Crates.** Ammo and health crates sit by the files out in the field, and a taken yard drops one of each.
- **Solid legs.** Gravity is heavier on the way down than up, so a jump arcs instead of floating; you can still jump for a moment after walking off a ledge, and a jump pressed just before landing goes off at touchdown. Land hard and you lose half your run unless you dive-roll it out; run into a wall and you slide along it. In the air you keep your momentum.
- **Soldier's moves.** Run, strafe, jump, crouch, go prone (`Z`: slow, steady, hard to spot). The dive-roll (`Shift`) is a dodge, not a way to travel: short, with a cooldown, and only its first stretch lets direct hits pass through you; strafe and roll to get sideways out from behind cover, and lying down it's a log roll. Cliffs stop you.
- **Peek round corners.** Hold `Q` or `E` to lean out left or right: your head, your muzzle and your hitbox go with it, and so does the camera, so you see and shoot round a wall's edge while most of you stays behind it. On your feet only, not prone or mid-roll.
- **The flow of a free-roaming action game.** Jump off anything; jump at a wall or a file whose top is within reach and you mantle up onto it; fall far enough and a parachute opens by itself. Above a few metres `Space` opens it by hand and `Space` again cuts it away, for a quick drop over a wall into the yard. Every compound has a stair up onto its wall walk too.
- **Headshots.** A round that lands above the shoulders does triple damage: the sniper takes a shielded soldier down with one. The crosshair rings orange and the feed says so.
- **Guards fight by their numbers.** The guards on you count themselves every beat and split the work (`regroup` in `game.ts`): one on its own is careful, keeps 14 to 26 metres off, fires short bursts, reloads from cover and falls back behind something when it's hurt; two split into a pinner and a flanker; three to five put a flanker round each side while the rest pin you, and the moment a flanker is sixty degrees round your side the whole squad pushes in; six to nine add a rush that comes straight at you and never stops for cover; ten or more just come. A pinner keeps the spot you ducked out of sight at under fire for three seconds to hold your head down while the others move, and a flanker holds its fire until it's round you or close, so its swing isn't announced. Down the pinner and its flanker counts itself again, alone, and turns careful. A big compound has a heavy: slower, tougher, with a launcher, and always the one pinning.
- **Ambushes.** A farm's odd guards lie flat behind the hedgerows along the lane in, unseen and unmoving until you're within eleven metres, a gun goes off within thirty, or they're hit; then they're up and the yard is up with them. The rest of the yard being alarmed doesn't rouse them.
- **Guards hunt gunfire.** A shot carries about 85 metres (further for the sniper, bazooka and shotgun; a stash is silent), and every guard that hears it comes looking toward where it was fired for a quarter of a minute, well past its yard. Camping out of sight brings them to you.
- **Missions.** *El Capitan*: carry a commit from the farthest yard back to HEAD (go down and you drop it). *The Backyard*: defend main for two minutes while the compounds raid it in waves; guards standing on it overwrite it.
- **Sound.** Every weapon has its own synthesized voice, panned by where it was fired. The cog's Controls panel turns effects up, down or off, and switches off gunfire, blasts, hits, shield, pickups or set-piece cues one by one.
- **Controls cog.** Look X and Y, touch X and Y, invert Y, brightness, camera zoom and tilt, and the sound pieces, saved between visits.
- **Your soldier.** A soft clay mannequin: fat rounded limbs, a ball for a head, no face. It kneels to crouch, lies flat prone, balls up for a dive-roll, tucks a knee in a jump and dips on landing, flails when a blast throws it, and puts its hands up when it goes down. Poses blend into each other; it strafes with its legs going sideways and backs up with the stride reversed, leans into a run, leans back to aim up, and jolts away from a hit.
- **The camera.** Over your right shoulder: aim is instant, but the camera eases after your body through a crouch or going prone, dips when you land, and widens while you run and in a dive-roll. It never goes through a wall or a file to show you what's behind it: the shoulder offset stops short of a wall beside you and the camera comes in along its line to your side of one behind you, snapping in so a wall never flashes through and easing back out (`cameraReach` in `game.ts`). The head is a slot: the plain ball, or a white block with a logo on its four sides (`src/ui/heads.ts`; a logo is a row there and an image in `public/heads/`). Builds are looks only, with one hitbox for all.
- **Plays on a phone.** On a touch screen a stick under the left thumb moves, a drag anywhere else aims, and buttons under the right thumb fire (hold), jump, dive-roll, crouch, reload and swap guns; tap the prompt by the crosshair to get in a vehicle or open the parachute. Held upright, the camera sits further back so the soldier doesn't fill the view.
- **Lives are the reflog.** Go down and you're back at the start, three times.
- **Battles.** Up to 64 players in one repo. *Side by side* is everyone against the guards on the level's mission; *Free-for-all* is first to 15 downs; *Teams* puts ours at HEAD and theirs at the far end, first side to 30, with the guards shooting at both. Battles run ten minutes, you're back three seconds after going down, and the next round starts on the same repo. Each player wears their own head and build; theirs are slate. Hold `Tab` for the scoreboard. Host one from the desktop app's menu (it shows an address and passphrase to give friends on your network), or Join someone's.
- **Fight any repo.** Type `owner/repo` on the menu and any public GitHub repo becomes the level, read in the browser at play time.
- **Four kinds of compound.** A level takes them in turn (`styleOf`), so every level has a mix. A *qalat* is the mud fort: thick curtain walls round the yard with a tower at each corner and one taller watchtower, a gate between pillars on the yard's easiest side (facing HEAD when that's as easy), an inner wall with a doorway splitting the yard in two, a stair up onto the wall walk, and low orchard walls outside the gate for cover on the way in. A *farm* is bocage: a waist-high field-stone ring you jump at to mantle over, a back way out, a hedgerow across the yard, hedgerows out along the lane in with the ambush behind them, and the biggest file as the barn. A *village* has no wall to speak of: short runs of garden wall all round with gaps between, sandbags either side of the way in and on the back corners, and the biggest file as a church tower over the square. A *strongpoint* is poured concrete, low and thick enough to mantle, an open gate, and a casemate across the back the files dig in low in front of. Everywhere the files are rooms built against the walls, a dozen or so a yard. Curtain walls can't be razed, so you go in through the gate, over the top, or down on a parachute.
- **Shelled ground.** A compound that was fought over lately (a fifth of the repo's churn), and two in five besides, has been shelled: a breach knocked through its back wall with the rubble piled low in the gap, a tower down to a stump, some of its rooms down to rubble (a low broken slab with a few lines left in it, cover to lie behind), shell holes in its yard and smoke hanging over the worst of it. More shell holes are blown across the field, with smoke over every third. Smoke is cover: nobody, you or a guard, sees through more than six metres of it, so a smoke bank is a way into a yard, and a blind one.
- **The campaign: a climb.** The levels are six chapters, from the bottom up: boot camp on the kitchen floor, holding the backyard, a lost commit in the garden, then real ground, the valley floor under El Capitan, the shoulders of Half Dome, and the heights at Glacier Point. Each chapter is a mission with a few lines of story about the launch codes somebody committed to main, and ends on command's debrief, won or lost; winning one opens the next (and offers it on the results screen). Battles and *fight anywhere* still play any level.
- **Set pieces, played in the game.** A chapter can carry scripted beats (`src/sim/director.ts`) that take the camera for a moment, slow time or hold some of your input, then hand back mid-fight, played straight with deadpan radio over the top. Boot camp opens on a helicopter bringing you in low over HEAD: jump when you like (`Space`), or it tips you out for a slow-motion drop, and leaves not quite in control. Merge your first compound and a strike throws you; your gun lands out of reach, you crawl to it half-blind and half-deaf, and a squad comes over the wall from the nearest compound still held, in slow motion. Then rally on the next one, or fall back to HEAD, where a health crate is waiting. The other chapters have their own: Hold the yard opens on a landing craft that brings you in across the lawn to HEAD (it's amphibious; grass counts), the ramp drops in slow motion and a squad opens up on the shore as you wade off; the roadster you board there leaves the ground and you bail out in a dive-roll (`Shift`) while the empty car carries on; a hovercraft beaches on the lawn in Lost commit and slowly deflates; the valley floor drops you from high up under a parachute with the camera on the wall, and the first time you close on a held compound there (and on the shoulders) command calls a fire mission on it, shells whistle in round its yard a beat apart and hurt whoever's in it, none within nine metres of you; on the shoulders the roadster's thrusters run dry in the air and it slides back in slow motion; the heights send two squads at once, then the last guard falls in slow motion and a rope ladder lifts you out before the result. The chapters that start on foot open on a slow, cheesy sweeping shot round you with a narrator's line (`Space` skips it). Between beats command chatters: a sitrep as each folder merges, a raid called in, a word when you're low. A first headshot, a reset crate and your last life each get a three-second git-joke cutaway. Each beat reads the level as it stands, so it fits whatever you've already blown up. Battles run none.
- **Ramps out of every gate.** A gate never opens onto a drop: it goes on the side with the easiest way out, and a ramp no steeper than an easy walk is graded out of it, cut into the hill or built up as a causeway, until it meets the ground. The footpath runs to the ramp's foot, other paths keep off it, and raiders on a defend mission come out through their gate and down the footpath instead of walking into their own walls.
- **Footpaths.** A trail is cut from HEAD to every compound's gate, graded so a soldier can always walk it, even up to a fort on high ground, so every compound can be reached on foot. A marker in the HUD says where to go next (the nearest compound still held, the commit, HEAD or main) and how far it is; out of view it sits at the screen's edge with an arrow toward it.
- **A richer map.** Folders that import each other are joined by roads. A folder that changed a lot lately holds more guards. Files agents mostly wrote are black glass, not paper. A failing check sets its folder's compound on fire until you merge it.
- **Toy heads.** You start with the ball, the logo blocks and five toy heads (the HTML5 and CSS3 shields, the JS and TS tiles, Cloudflare), drawn close to the real marks but not quite. Every level you win (a level on a repo you haven't won it on) unlocks another: cartoony takes on Bun, Rust (a generic cog), Three.js, React, Alchemy, Effect, Tailwind, Better Auth, Vite, TanStack, Svelte, Vue and Nuxt.

## Run it

```
cd apps/git-recon
bun install
bun run dev            # the browser
bun run desktop:dev    # the desktop app
bun test               # terrain, the repo mapping, live loading, heads, movement, weapons, crates, guards, missions, the campaign, battles
```

A battle in dev: `bun run battle` serves the world on 127.0.0.1:4180 (Vite proxies `/ws` to it), then `bun run dev`. Host in one tab and Join with no address in another: each tab is its own player.

### Battles online

`bun run edge:dev` runs the browser build behind a Cloudflare Worker locally (`wrangler dev`); `bun run deploy` deploys it. `src/edge/worker.ts` serves the page and sends `/ws?room=CODE` to that room's Durable Object, the same `World` class the desktop hosts. The Worker vouches each player's id and name, refuses WebSockets from other sites and limits how many new rooms one address opens a minute (per isolate: a speed bump, not a quota). Online the menu deals in room codes: Host makes one and puts it in the link (`?room=CODE`) to send friends; Join takes one. Everyone is a guest, so whoever picks the battle first owns the room and only they can pick again. The page sends the repo with its pick; a load keeps at most 360 files and 24 excerpts, so even a big repo's setup is about 220 KB at most, well within the world's 1 MB cap (`tests/edge.test.ts` checks the worst case).

What a room costs (Workers Paid, priced 2026-10-12 from [Cloudflare's DO pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)): the world steps 20 times a second while anyone's in, so the object never hibernates and is billed for 128 MB the whole time: 460.8 GB-s an hour, $0.0058 at $12.50 per million GB-s. Each player's inputs come in about 30 a second, billed at 20:1 as requests: 5,400 an hour, $0.0008 at $0.15 per million. Snapshots going out (binary deltas, about 25 KB/s a player in a full room) aren't billed: outgoing messages and Workers bandwidth are free. So a full room of 64 is about **$0.06 an hour**, and the plan's included 400,000 GB-s and 1M requests cover about 870 room-hours and 185 player-hours a month. A room ends once everyone has left, and closes after five minutes with no input from anyone, so a tab left open in the background doesn't keep one billed.

## How it works

- `packages/terrain` is the ground: Park Hide & Seek's heightmap format, or Druids Curse's noise and stamps with an optional water table, and `withPads()` to cut a level shelf where a compound stands.
- `src/levels/place.ts` turns a lat/long into that heightmap format in the page: it fetches the Terrarium tiles (they're served with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`), inflates the PNGs with the browser's `DecompressionStream` and samples a 2 km square. The tests run it on a recorded tile.
- `src/sim/codemap.ts` lays a repo onto the ground, like codeSplitter's code city lays repos onto streets. Files are grouped by folder (`apps/x` and `packages/x` count as their own); the biggest six become compounds, placed on flat ground you can walk to from the start, as far from each other as they fit. Each is built in one of four styles taken in turn (qalat, farm, village, strongpoint), all from wall blocks told apart by what they're made of (mud, stone, hedge, sandbag, concrete), with a gate on its easiest side and a ramp graded out of it, the files as rooms against the walls, and a graded footpath cut from HEAD to the ramp's foot. A shelled compound gets a breach, rubble rooms, shell holes (craters stamped into the finished ground with `withStamps`) and a smoke patch; more holes and smoke go out in the field. Files from everywhere else are crates in the field. Imports between compounds become roads, a folder's share of recent changes adds up to two guards, a file whose recent commits were mostly agents' is glass, and a failing check whose name has a folder's name in it sets that compound on fire.
- `src/sim/intel.ts` works out what a snapshot knows besides sizes: imports between folders (relative ones, and workspace packages by their `package.json` name), churn per file over recent commits, and which of those an agent made (a `Co-Authored-By` trailer or a bot author).
- `src/levels/github.ts` loads any public repo: the repo, its last ten commits and their files, its tree, the check runs at the tip (about a dozen of the 60 API calls an hour GitHub gives a browser), plus the biggest scripts' text from `raw.githubusercontent.com` for imports. A load is kept in the browser for half an hour.
- `src/sim/heads.ts` lists the toy heads, the starters and what unlocks the rest; `src/ui/characters.ts` builds each from a few primitives, and `src/ui/heads.ts` puts them after the plain and logo heads on the menu.
- `src/sim/weapons.ts` is the arsenal, one table row per git command: damage, splash, speed, gravity, ammo, homing, pellets, bloom, burn, how hard it wrecks files, recoil, and an effect (revert, stash, blame, reset) that `game.ts` carries out where the round lands.
- `src/sim/game.ts` is the whole game with no rendering: movement and stances, shots, splash, files losing lines and coming down, burning, crates, stashes, the guards (they watch, spot you or a stash, wait a beat, count the squad on you and take a job in it: pin, flank or rush, by `tacticFor`; shoot in bursts and take cover behind files; an ambush waits flat) and the rules. Smoke blocks sight (`smoked`), and `cameraReach` is what keeps the page's camera out of walls. Each guard keeps a three-second trail of where it has been, which is what `git revert` rewinds.
- `src/sim/roles.ts` is the four kits; `Game.arm()` hands one out, and a respawn takes up the role picked for the next life.
- `src/sim/vehicles.ts` is the killstreak vehicles' numbers; `game.ts` drops them, drives them (a roadster steers like a car on the ground and flies on its thrusters, a helicopter strafes where you look within a ceiling over the ground) and routes hits on the driver to the vehicle.
- A battle is the same `Game` with a mode (`coop`, `ffa` or `teams`): players join after the guards and take a left player's slot, guards go for the nearest player they can see, `hostile()` says who hurts whom, and downs score.
- `src/net/room.ts` runs a battle with no sockets: it keeps each player's latest input (and any tap since the last step, so a quick click still fires), steps the game, and packs each player a snapshot of only what's within 90 m of them (the events every round makes, too), as a delta against the last snapshot that player acked: only the soldiers that changed, and only their fields that did; a round in flight once, then again only if it strays from the line the page flies it on. `src/net/wire.ts` is what crosses the socket: the welcome as JSON, snapshots as binary frames (your ammo and clocks, and the events, binary too: a shot fired is 4 bytes). On a 32-player test a delta averages under 500 bytes, about 9 KB/s a player (the JSON snapshots before were about 3 KB, 60 KB/s). The page acks with each input, and each snapshot echoes the newest input the world had. `src/net/world.ts` is the room as a Durable Object-shaped world: the host player's page sends the setup (level, mode, repo, and a lat/long level's ground, checked to be n*n heights on a real place), everyone joining gets it in their welcome and builds the same map, then the world steps and sends snapshots 20 times a second. At 64 players a second of battle steps and packs in about a fifth of a second of CPU.
- Online, `src/edge/worker.ts` is the Worker in front of a `World` Durable Object per room code, and `ONLINE` in `wrangler.jsonc` turns on the world's online rules (first to pick owns the room, idle rooms close). See [Battles online](#battles-online).
- `src/net/mirror.ts` is the page's copy of a battle: everyone else is drawn 100 ms behind the newest snapshot, eased between the two either side; your own soldier moves on your keys at once (`Game.predict`). Each input goes out numbered, and a snapshot echoes the newest number the world had: the page puts your soldier where the world has it, plays your frames since on top, and eases there (or jumps, past 3 m), so a correction never fights a strafe. Shots are the world's call, but each input also says what time the page drew everyone else at, and the world checks that player's rounds against where soldiers were then (up to 300 ms back), so you aim where you see them, not ahead by your lag. It keeps the frames it built from recent snapshots, since the next delta builds on one of them. `src/ui/net.ts` is the socket, and `src/host/host.ts` tells the page this machine's LAN address.
- `src/sim/campaign.ts` orders the levels into the climb, with each chapter's story, and says which are open from the wins in the browser.
- `src/sim/missions.ts` says what wins each level (clear, carry or defend); `game.ts` keeps the mission's state in `objective`.
- `src/ui/figure.ts` builds the soldiers from rounded limbs on joints (inked by pushing each mesh's back faces out along its normals) and poses them from what each one is doing: stance, how it's moving against where it faces, recoil, a hit's jolt, a landing. Given a blend rate it eases every joint towards the pose, so moves flow into each other; `tests/figure.test.ts` checks the poses land where they should.
- `src/ui/` draws it in codeSplitter's paper style (contours spaced for each level's relief, rock faces hatched), feeds it the mouse and keys, and plays its events through `audio.ts`, a synthesized voice per weapon in Park Hide & Seek's style.

The repo is a snapshot: `bun scripts/snapshot.ts <repo-dir> <name>` writes the path and line count of its biggest tracked source files to `src/levels/repos/<name>.json`, with the imports between folders, the last hundred commits' churn, and the opening lines of the biggest scripts (`excerpts()` in `src/sim/intel.ts`) that the ground is printed with, and, when its origin is on GitHub and `gh` is signed in, the checks failing at HEAD, which set their compounds on fire. The levels use this repo, Rusty Buns itself.

## Editing the script

The game's words (radio, gags and stories) live in `content/*.json`. `bun run cms` serves them as textareas on localhost and saves back to the files; reload the game to see them.
