# Skate Tech Park

> **What it proves:** a three.js game whose physics is a pure fixed-step sim, so the same code runs in the page and in headless tests, with no backend and a ten-line desktop config. One of the [Rusty Buns](../../README.md#see-it-work) examples.

A street skating spot at golden hour: a kicker, a funbox, a rail, and a gap no board can clear. It is one board with one physics. Ride it as a skateboard, or press Tab and the same board wears the rocket roadster: a low white wedge with ten cold-gas thrusters. They fire in time with what the board does, as if the rockets did the tricks: the four at the back on a push, a pump or a boost, the four under the corners on a pop and (unevenly) through a flip, the two at the nose on a spin, and a short burst on a landing. Tab changes only how it looks, never how it rides.

Tricks score when you land them: air time, every 180 you spin, kickflips, grinds, and riding away fakie or in reverse. Land sideways or mid-flip and you bail: you keep your momentum, slide and tumble across the ground, then stand back up.

## Jump to the state

`/scenarios.html` builds a link for any state and lists all 63 parks one tap each. The same keys work on the game's own address, and agents get them as `skate_go`, `skate_start` and `skate_perf`.

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `?level=<id>` | the park |
| `?spawn=rail` / `balance` / `vert` | start 14 m before the nearest bar, before the longest gentle bar, or 12 m from the tallest rideable wall, facing it (or any spawn name) |
| `?quality=low\|mid\|high\|auto` | force a tier (remembered; `auto` forgets) |
| `?weak[=ms]` | burn that many ms a frame (45 by default) like a slow GPU; start on `high` and watch it step down |
| `?fps` | frame-time readout with the live tier (always on with `?weak`) |
| `?controls=touch\|desktop` | show the thumb stick and flick pad and let a mouse drag them, or hide them on a phone |

## The trick compendium

A flick trick is data (`src/tricks.ts`): how far the board turns under the rider and which way it flips, the same grid [SkateBench](https://github.com/T3-Content/skatebench) (MIT) asks models to name. Nine tricks: kickflip (`F`), heelflip (`G`), shove-it (`V`), varial (`R`), varial heel (`O`), hardflip (`U`), inward heel (`I`), tre flip (`T`) and laser flip (`Y`). With the mouse or right stick the diagonals are the compound ones (up-right varial, up-left varial heel, down-right hardflip, down-left inward heel) and a long diagonal drag down is a double turn. A tre flip with a half turn of the rider on top lands as a big flip. Where the deck lands on the bar and the approach angle name ten grinds (50-50, 5-0, nosegrind, smith, feeble, crooked, boardslide, lipslide, tailslide, noseslide), on rails and ledges alike; the tail or nose tip over the bar still catches. Balance is a lean you hold, on grinds and manuals: each grind rests over a different amount and tips at its own rate, speed steadies it, a sloped bar pulls it, and the stick takes a moment to act, so it rocks before it settles. A small meter under the combo shows it (`src/grinds.ts`).

## Flick the tricks on a phone

On a touch screen one thumb drives the stick and the other flicks on a board drawn on the flick pad, nose up. Like footprint grip tape, it shows where the fingers go and a dotted path for each stroke out of them, named at its tip; orange paths pop. The path a stroke matched lights up. Start on the nose to nollie (`N` on the keyboard): the front foot pops it, the back foot levels it, and it lands as "nollie kickflip" and the like.

The cog (top right of the HUD) opens the Controls: look and touch speeds (the stick's X and Y trims included), invert Y, brightness, camera zoom and tilt, and the pad's own settings. They are saved in the browser. The game makes no sound, so there are no audio rows.

Which stroke does which trick is a flick profile (`src/flick.ts`): a list of strokes, each "from the nose, tail or anywhere, this way (or a tap), at least this far" and what it does (pop an ollie or a nollie, flick a trick, or both), plus which half of the screen is the pad and how long a still press waits before it is a manual. `src/flick-profiles.ts` holds twenty: classic and its goofy mirror, fingerboard (tap the tail to ollie, flick off the nose's edges), short-thumb and long-throw sizes, tap pop, compass, nollie first, pull pop, one swipe, nose flicks, tail scoop, first five, fine lines, quick and slow manuals, and lefty pads. The footprints button under the pad steps through them and the browser keeps the pick. `?flick=<name>` picks one, and `?flick=` with a profile as JSON (or base64url JSON) rides one of your own; anything it leaves out comes from classic.

## Rails bend and drop

`railLine` (`src/levels/pieces.ts`) extrudes one rail along a path with a profile (`kink`, `curve`, `s`, `wave`), and a grind rides on from segment to segment round the bend or down the drop. The view draws a bent bar as one smooth tube through its joints, with a post only every few metres. `embedded.ts` sets long smooth bars and steel-edged ledges into the hillsides of every park (3 or 4 each), laid on the finished ground so they sit flush: one that follows a slope's contour, one that dips to the earth mid-run and climbs back out, a ledge cut along a bank, and a bar that leaves the hill and stands clear on posts where the ground falls away. Each lifts clear of the ground at both ends so it can be ollied onto, and a rider grinds it from end to end. How we plan to fill out the levels, what mobile has today and a chasing animal for timed runs are in [LEVEL_DESIGN.md](LEVEL_DESIGN.md).

## Weather

Every park has its own air (`weather` in its spec, moods in `src/render/weather.ts`): drifting clouds and a sun disc in the sky, fog that closes in, grain on the ground, rain streaks that lean into the wind, snow that whitens the flats, dust, mist, drifting motes, and lightning in a storm. Yosemite Falls is rainy, Half Dome snowy, the Grand Canyon dusty. Add `?weather=rain|storm|snow|dust|mist|noon|dusk|overcast|golden` to any park to change it.

## The whole park is the skate park

Beyond the hand-placed kit, every park is filled with contour ribbons (`src/levels/ribbons.ts`): a ribbon traces the hillside at one height and extrudes a profile along it (a berm, a cut bank, a channel, rollers, whoop berms) as a chain of short pieces with coping along the crest, so it follows the land instead of standing on it. A profile is a recipe with a width and a height, so one berm molds into a low roller or a tall wall, and `add`, `mirror` and `waves` make new profiles from old ones. `fill: { lines: 8 }` in a `ParkSpec` lays them across the slopes the sites leave empty, clear of spawns, gaps, trees and each other.

## Edge to edge, with a wall you can see

A park spans all of its terrain (about 640 m across, `edgeOf` in `src/levels/park.ts`), and ribbons, kit and earthworks fill the whole of it. Its edge is a paper-white wall with orange chevrons at the foot and an orange post every 24 m (`src/render/barrier.ts`). It stands exactly where the sim stops the board, and glows solid as you close on it.

## The park is cut into the terrain

The hand-placed kit and ribbons stand on the ground; the earthworks (`src/levels/earthworks.ts`) *are* the ground. Bowls and pools are dug into the meadow, a pump track of rollers and a row of dirt jumps are packed on top, spillway channels are cut along the slope, and earth quarter pipes and mounds are piled up. Kidney pools, a keyhole (round end and square bay), a three-terrace pit with a lip to grind on each step, and deep vert bowls, cradles and storm drains are cut too; granite ridges and slabs stand with ledges along their crests to grind. On a hillside (slope 0.12 to 0.6, fitted over 24 m by `hillPlane`) the works are cut relative to the sloped ground instead, yawed so uphill is local +z, with lips at the hill's own height, level floors and coping on every lip: a quarter pipe dug into the uphill side (`hillQuarter`), a half pipe across the fall line with the uphill wall taller (`hillHalf`), a planar bank steeper than the hill, or with a hip that juts down the hill (`hillBank`), a bermed snake run winding down the fall line (`hillSnake`) and a drop-in shelf with a quarter at its foot (`hillShelf`). Poured concrete is swept clear of snow, so the cuts read on Half Dome. Every park has a cut within 60 m of a spawn: `layOut` looks round the spawns for one, and a park too flat for that gets a "hillside cut" spawn below the nearest (`hillSpawn`); a park with little hill lowers `hillMin` in its `earthworks` spec. Each kind is cut about as often as the others in a park (the kind with the fewest so far goes first), and a park holds at most `hillMax` (36) of them so a mountain park keeps its level kit. `scripts/ride.ts` rides each from below. Level works are each held at the height of the ground under their centre and blended back into the hillside, so the sim, camera, mesh and map see one heightfield. `layOut` tries every cell of the map for one (crowded meadows get the small ones), clear of spawns, gap lines, trees and landmarks; lips carry coping you can grind. The ground mesh is 2 m fine, painted concrete or hardpack with a pale coping stone along each lip, and shades hollows. `earthworks: false` in a `ParkSpec` turns it off.

## The board, the kit and the roadster are modelled in Blender

`blender/fingerboard.py` builds the fingerboard as a real mesh, at the built board's size: a pressed deck whose round ends are squeezed onto a disk instead of pinched to a point, bevelled ply, grip with bolt heads, shaped hangers, stacked bushings, crowned wheels on cores. It then decimates that mesh to three polygon budgets: `hi` (about 26k triangles), `mid` (6k, the default) and `low` (1.5k). `bun run models` writes them to `public/models/` (`blender -b --factory-startup -P blender/fingerboard.py -- --render out.png` adds a three-quarter render and an underside render to judge it by eye). The game loads one with `?board=hi|mid|low`, and `?board=built` keeps the three.js board. Each material is named after a parts-picker slot, so the picker paints the model.

`blender/kit.py` models the kit the same way: a wooden ramp (riding sheets with dark joints, rounded trim, ply sides on a frame of ribs, a steel plate where it meets the ground), a concrete stair (steel nosing over a recessed riser, cheek walls) and a concrete block. A piece's surface is its own height function, often draped over real ground, so each module is a unit box that `src/render/kit.ts` fits to the piece: its top follows that function, so what you see is what you ride, and its edge bands keep their size in metres. Ramps (kicker, quarter pipe, funbox, spine, tabletop, gap landings), stair sets and boxes and ledges wear it; earthworks keep the sampled mesh. The landscape pieces have modules too, stretched evenly over their whole shape: a granite boulder on rings that close in towards its foot, with mottled granite and lichen; a fallen log with furrowed bark, moss and ringed end grain where it tapers to the ground; and a wooden hip whose sheets are cut along its four creases, each with an orange trim strip. Two more: `rockFace`, a three-tier granite buttress that curves up to a sheer lip with coping on each tier (a `cliff` module: a jittered square grid in granite and lichen), and the poured bowl (a `bowl` module: saw-cut panel joints, with a tile band near the lip and a darker floor shaded by height in `kit.ts`). Where a piece has no height (a boulder's corners) nothing is drawn. `?kit=hi|mid|low` picks the budget (`mid` by default) and `?kit=built` turns it off. Park furniture too: a giant picnic table (`picnicTable`, a `table` module: planks with grooves between, bolt heads, A-frame legs and a cross brace) whose top is a box to manual across and grind along, with a seat either side as a low ledge of its own (`bench`), so the seats are ridden too; the training plaza has one in its quiet corner. And a boardwalk (`boardwalk`, a `boardwalk` module: planks laid across the walk with grooves between, kerb boards, stringers and posts), a raised plank walk to manual along with a ledge down each edge and a handrail a metre over the deck to grind, round the spring at Grand Prismatic, the geyser at Old Faithful and between the named trees in Mariposa Grove. `kit.html` on the dev server is the kit room: one of each piece and the board, under a circling camera (`?at=2` frames the stairs, `?at=9` to `11` the boulder, the log and the hip, `?at=12` and `?at=13` the rock face and the pool, `?at=14` the picnic table and, behind the row, the scenery set).

`blender/landmark.py` models Half Dome as a far landmark: a granite massif with a sheer west face, dark streaks, a talus apron, a rounded shoulder and forest at its foot (about 8k triangles at `mid`, 23k `hi`, 2.4k `low`). It is a backdrop standing past the park's west edge (a `peak` landmark in `half-dome.ts`, drawn by `src/render/peak.ts`), so the dome reads from the spawn through snow and fog: its materials ignore the fog and take only a share of the haze. `?peak=hi|mid|low` picks the budget (`mid` by default) and `?peak=off` hides it. `?spawn=view` starts the ride at the park's view spawn, facing the peak (any spawn name works). `blender/landmarks.py` adds four more far landmarks for the parks beyond the Sierra, built the same way (crest at 1, foot on 0, `model` in the `peak` landmark picks one): a free-standing sandstone `arch` on a slickrock pedestal with one fat leg and one slender, an amphitheatre of banded `hoodoos` stepped where a harder layer caps a softer one, a flat-topped `mesa` with sheer fluted walls, a pale cap and a talus apron, and a snow-capped `volcano` whose glaciers run further down its valleys than its ridges.

`blender/scenery.py` models the scenery strewn over a park, the toys that make the board read as giant: the pine (ruffled tiers), the aspen, the stump, the rock, the cairn, the trail sign, the bush, the fern, the wildflowers and the tent, and six more for the parks beyond the Sierra: a joshua tree, a saguaro, a palm with arching fronds, an oak, a snag (a dead bare trunk) and sage. All sixteen go in one GLB per budget (`scenery-hi|mid|low.glb`, a tree is 900, 320 or 110 triangles) and `loadScenery` in `src/render/scenery.ts` bakes each material colour into vertex colours, so a kind is still one instanced mesh. `?scenery=hi|mid|low` picks the budget (`mid` by default) and `?scenery=built` keeps the primitives. Every one of these budgets defaults to the quality tier: `?quality=low|mid|high` (`packages/util/src/quality.ts`) sets the pixel ratio, antialias, shadows and model budgets together, is remembered, and is guessed from the GPU the first time (an old integrated chip or a software renderer starts on `low`); a ride that stays under 24 fps for a few seconds steps itself down a tier, and `?quality=auto` forgets the choice. A park's `biome` (`pine`, `desert`, `hardwood`, `swamp` or `alpine`, in its `ParkSpec`) says what grows on it: the desert has joshua trees, saguaros, sage and rock where the meadow would be; `?biome=desert` previews any park that way. In the kit room, `?at=14` frames it behind the picnic table at 3 m.

`blender/roadster.py` models the rocket roadster as the October 2026 reveal shows it, not the 2017 prototype: a body lofted through cross-sections into flat facets with hard creases and a chine down each flank, Ano White over a dark sill, a gold visor canopy, dual triangular headlights, a plain tail light bar over the diffuser that holds the four rear nozzles, arches cut round the wheels, and the pop-out wing up on its struts, at the shared roadster's footprint. `dressRoadster` swaps it in over the shared one and keeps that one's ten nozzle bells and its hinged plate, so the plumes and the plate drop still work. `?car=hi|mid|low` (`mid` by default, about 1.5k triangles; the budgets differ in how round the wheels and arches are) or `?car=built`; `?showroom` parks it under a circling camera.

## The title screen

A bare address opens on the logo: a redwood whose trunk runs down into the end of a skateboard deck, bark furrows carrying on through the board, with trucks and wheels under it (`src/front/logo.ts`). The intro drifts from the canopy down the trunk like a nature film, then "SKATE TECH PARK" is skated in left to right and the menu opens: Single (free skate, run from the bear) or Multi (free skate in a room, or join a code). Any key or click skips the film; reduced motion goes straight to the menu. A choice adds `?mode=` (and `room`/`chase`), and the park picker carries those through (`carry` in `src/front/menu.ts`), so every old link that names a level or room skips the title. Multi also lists Race (first through the finish, landing a set trick inside a ring) and S.K.A.T.E. (set a trick, the next rider lands it or takes a letter), greyed until they are built.

## The home page flies over a park

With no level in the address there is no rider: a slow camera tours a park chosen at random (`?tour=half-dome` picks one, `?at=40` starts forty seconds in) behind the title menu and the park picker, whose text sits in rounded, 80% opaque panels. It sweeps round the spawn, the park's landmark and its biggest pieces from a high, narrow, near-isometric angle, then flies up the hill to the highest ground for one wide pan over everything (`src/render/tour.ts`, a pure function of time). It is the live game scene, so nothing is recorded or served as video. On the picker, **Ride** starts the park on show, **Next park** flies over another, and a card per park starts that one.

## An agent can ride it

For fun: in a browser with WebMCP (`document.modelContext`), the page hands an agent the board. `src/agent.ts` registers
tools that hold inputs the way keys do and answer with what the board did: `skate_look` (where it is, speed, what it's
doing, score and combo, the ground and rails ahead), `skate_ride` (one move held for some seconds), `skate_line` (code
mode: a whole run as a list of moves in one call), `skate_snapshot` (a 640 px JPEG of the chase camera), `skate_reset`,
`skate_swap`, `skate_parks` and `skate_go`. The moves go through the same step as the keyboard, so an agent rides the
same sim a player does. Nothing registers without modelContext.

```js
await skate_line({ moves: [{ push: 1, seconds: 3 }, { ollie: true, seconds: 0.15 }, { trick: "tre flip", seconds: 2 }] })
// { tricks: [{ name: "tre flip", points: 743 }], now: { kmh: 55, doing: "rolling", ahead: { rails: [...] }, ... } }
await skate_snapshot() // { image: "data:image/jpeg;base64,..." }
```

## Ride together, on Cloudflare

The users button (or `?room=ABCD`) opens a room of up to four riders on the level you are on; the link is the invite. Everyone rides their own sim and sees the others as boards with a name tag, and you can run into them (`src/net/bump.ts`: pushed out and bounced). The shield button switches the room to PvE (`&pve=1`), where boards pass through each other; a room is one mode, so it is a different room from the same code with collisions. The server only relays poses, so it costs next to nothing idle. `src/edge/worker.ts` serves the built page and sends `/ws` to a `World` Durable Object per level and room code (`src/edge/world.ts`, rules in `src/net/room.ts`, the cap is `MAX_RIDERS`). Where there is no `/ws` (the dev server, the desktop app) it stays single player.

```
bun run edge:dev         # the whole thing locally, with wrangler
bun run deploy           # to Cloudflare (the owner runs this: it needs the account)
```

## Paying for finishes

Priced deck, truck and wheel finishes (looks only) are bought through Stripe Checkout (`src/edge/pay.ts`). The page asks `/api/checkout` for a hosted payment page; the Worker prices the item from the catalog, never from the page. A purchase is recorded only when Stripe's signed webhook (`/api/stripe-webhook`) says it was paid, into a `Purchases` Durable Object per player id, and the page reads it back from `/api/owned`. Players are guests: the id is the secret one kept in the browser, so clearing site data loses the purchases until accounts exist. The dev server has no Worker, so there the free test checkout is used; the desktop app can't buy priced finishes.

Payments are behind a flag: unless the build sets `VITE_PAYMENTS=1`, priced finishes show faded and locked as "coming soon" and can't be clicked, so the app can be shown to people without taking money.

The owner sets this up once (agents don't touch secrets): put `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` (a restricted key that can create Checkout Sessions) and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` on the Worker as secrets, through varlock and 1Password, then `bun run deploy`. In the Stripe dashboard add a webhook endpoint `https://<your worker>/api/stripe-webhook` for the event `checkout.session.completed`, and try it in test mode first with card `4242 4242 4242 4242`.

`/?showroom` parks the roadster and circles the camera round it, to check the model against reference photos.

## How it's built

- `src/level.ts`: the `Level` the sim rides on: a height at any point, pieces, rails, spawns and bounds. `src/levels/plaza.ts` is the training plaza, but the game opens in a national park (El Capitan Meadow) unless `?level=` says otherwise. The view meshes each piece by sampling its own height.
- `src/levels/`: skate parks on real ground. `pieces.ts` is the kit (kicker, quarter pipe, bowl, funbox, ledge, handrail, flat bar, sky rail (an impossible bar on stilts, 100 to 160 m long, fed by a kicker, in every park; a bar climbing faster than you fall still catches you from below, so steep ones land: it's a tech deck, the landscape is the toy), stairs, hubba, hip, spine, and a named `gap`), plus landscape features a snow park would cut from a mountain: a creek `halfpipe`, a `tabletop`, `rollers` to pump, a `fallenLog` with a grind along its top, an `outcrop` boulder, a `rockFace` (granite that curves up to vertical in three tiers, each with coping) and a `geyserCone` with a crater. Each is a pure height function plus rail segments. `park.ts` shrinks a [park zone](../../packages/terrain) by `TERRAIN_SCALE` (a 1 km zone becomes a ~333 m disc, squashed vertically so cliff faces are friendly to ride; `?real` or the mountain button gives the true height), drapes each site's kit onto the slope (a site can ask for a flat pad instead), and carries each level's gaps, goals, pickups and spawns as data. The ground makes the big features itself: a gap can be just where you leave the ground and where you land, and a goal can be to climb a cliff face. `goals.ts` watches a run (gap bonuses, combo, pickups). Levels: El Capitan Meadow, Glacier Point Rim, Old Faithful Bowl, Mariposa Grove, Half Dome, Yosemite Falls, Grand Prismatic, Grand Canyon, then Zion, Arches, Bryce Canyon, Rocky Mountain, Grand Teton, Glacier, Olympic, Joshua Tree, Death Valley and Crater Lake, then Acadia, Badlands, Big Bend, Black Canyon, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, Carlsbad Caverns, Great Sand Dunes, Great Smoky Mountains, Haleakala, Hawaii Volcanoes, Mount Rainier, Lassen Volcanic, Sequoia and Redwood, then the other 35, from American Samoa to Wrangell-St. Elias: a place in every one of the 63 US national parks. Where the tiles carry the sea or a lake floor (the islands, the fjords, Voyageurs, Indiana Dunes) the table's `water` level flattens it to a floor at the shore; where the real ground is nearly level (Biscayne, the Everglades, White Sands) the named gap is poured from the kit instead of found in the land. `bun scripts/check.ts src/levels/<id>.ts` runs the tests' checks on one park file before it is wired in; the slow per-park journeys in the suite (`ride.test.ts`, `tricklist.test.ts`) ride a sample of the parks, every one with `ALL_PARKS=1`. `src/levels/parks.ts` is that list as data (state, the lat/lon of the park's signature spot, a blurb, the weather, the scenery biome); the picker groups its cards by region, with a filter strip. A zone is not bundled: `loadZone` (packages/terrain) imports its json when the level is first built, so a page carries only the ground it rides. A new place is three steps: `bun scripts/fetch-parks.ts <id>` writes the 2 km of real ground for a table row (about nine seconds, any spot the public elevation tiles cover; `packages/terrain/scripts/fetch-place.ts` does it for any lat/lon), `bun scripts/survey.ts <id>` prints a height map (`--relief` and `--bench N` try other heights and other benches), the flat benches, the drops a launch can fly off (found by riding them with the sim) and a plan, and a short file in `src/levels/` turns the plan into a park with `streetPark` (`template.ts`): a street line, a sky rail, a bowl, a named gap and a spawn on level ground facing the signature spot, plus whatever sites you add. Add `peak: { model, tall, y }` and `farPeak` stands one of the far landmarks straight ahead of the view spawn, past the park's edge with its toe on it (Arches, Bryce Canyon, Zion, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, Mount Rainier, Lassen Volcanic, Haleakala and Hawaii Volcanoes have one; `y` is the ground at that edge).
- `src/sim.ts`: the ride as a pure fixed-step simulation (60 steps a second), with no three.js or DOM. It handles grounded rolling with slopes and walls, launching off lips, air with spins and flips, grinds, landings and bails, and which thrusters are firing, derived from the board's state each step. The board keeps its energy: speed is along the surface and every height gained or lost is paid from it (v² - 2gh), so a halfpipe rides wall to wall. Vert reads the face at the lip itself, not the last stride's average: off a vertical lip the board airs straight up and comes back down onto the face, fakie unless it spun 180, and drops past the coping instead of catching it; pushed up to the lip with little to spare it rolls over onto the deck instead. Held push pumps a transition, so a halfpipe keeps going wall after wall, and an ollie high on a steep wall drives the board on up the face for a bigger air instead of throwing it out over the deck; a landing keeps the part of the velocity along the ground, so a drop into a transition rolls away fast; the wheels rolling and the air slow it gently; a sloping rail speeds a slide up or stalls it; a carve's turn carries into the air as spin.
- `src/models.ts`: the giant fingerboard (no rider; a dished, kicked deck with a round outline, ply edge and grip, trucks with baseplate, kingpin and bushing, urethane wheels on cores; `src/parts.ts` is the catalogue and `src/parts-picker.ts` the wrench-button picker that repaints it) and the roadster from three.js primitives, so there is nothing to load or license. The roadster's body is a side profile extruded across its width. `src/render/skins.ts` rides either one on the same pose and swaps them on Tab; `src/render/plumes.ts` draws the exhaust out of each nozzle. `src/render/chase.ts` is the chase camera, `src/render/sky.ts` the painted-looking sky (a rosy band between haze and blue, a wide warm sun wash) and four layers of fir-toothed far ridges that fade into the haze, Firewatch-style, `src/render/pieces.ts` meshes every piece by sampling its own height function (painted wood, concrete, colour-blocked edges) and every rail, ledge edge and coping in steel with grind wax, two draw calls a level; and `src/render/tilt.ts` tips the board up on two wheels in a manual, shaking as the balance runs out; `src/render/scenery.ts` the small pines, rocks, aspens, stumps, cairns, trail signs, tents, bushes, ferns and wildflowers that give a park its scale: the kit sits in a flowery clearing and the forest thickens into groves past its edge (they shrink away as the board nears). `src/render/critters.ts` the wildlife: birds that burst into the air, rabbits and squirrels that bolt, and gliders wheeling overhead; they run from the rider and the camera and are never caught.
- `src/levels/scatter.ts`: fills every park with kit parts from edge to edge (two seeded, jittered sweeps over the bounds, the second half a cell over; flat ground only; clear of sites, trees, landmarks, spawns and gap lines). A `ParkSpec` gets it by default; `scatter: { seed, cell, fill, slope }` tunes it, `scatter: false` turns it off.
- `src/levels/thumb.ts`: the picture on each start-screen card, the level's own floor sampled into a small hillshaded, contoured height map with a survey grid; the card tilts it against the pointer for a little parallax.
- `src/shop.ts`: pay to play, for looks only. A few finishes in `parts.ts` carry a `price` (cents) and show a lock in the picker until bought; no part changes how the board rides. A `Wallet` holds what you own and takes money through a `Checkout`. The page ships a test checkout that says yes and charges nothing, and keeps the wallet in this browser. Real payments need a backend: a Worker that talks to the payment provider and holds the wallet against an account.
- `src/main.ts`: the scene, the chase camera, the keys and the HUD.
