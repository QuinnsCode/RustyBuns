# Mythic Masters

> **What it proves:** a 3D arena brawler whose balance is a number, not a feeling: a deterministic 60 Hz sim that a headless bot-vs-bot tuner plays thousands of times a second. One of the [Rusty Buns](../../README.md#see-it-work) examples; all of them are in [EXAMPLES.md](../../EXAMPLES.md).

**Live:** https://mythic-masters.notryanquinn.workers.dev · on [eqosys](https://eqosys.qntbr.com)

A creature arena brawler where size and weight are real, fought by 76 beasts from folklore around the world, at least one for each of its 64 regions (the last 12 are small, common cryptids: the Snallygaster, the Dover Demon, the Hoop Snake, the Drop Bear and eight more; a 6 kg jackalope, the Jersey Devil on bat wings, a hodag and an agropelter are among them), each with six moves out of its legend on fighting-game inputs. Mass, height and six stats (the radar chart on each card) give each body its speed, turn rate, jump, stamina, armour and how far it flies when hit. So the small one can win by staying behind a slow turner, and the big one by landing a single heavy hit. The plan and the rules behind it are in [notes/game-ideas/1-creature-arena.md](../../notes/game-ideas/1-creature-arena.md).

- **Nothing hides in the air.** Flying drains stamina until you fall, faster the heavier the wings (a 4 kg wolpertinger stays up about ten seconds, the 900 kg roc under three), and a take-off needs half your stamina back, so every flyer has to land and stand a while. Only the Mothman soars as long as it likes. The ceiling is low, everyone's launcher (heavy with jump held) reaches up, a column pitches up at a flyer, and a heavy hit on a flyer clips its wings for three seconds.
- **The dive.** A flyer's heavy with fly-down held drops on the target. One that lands bounces back up out of reach; one that misses leaves the flyer on the ground beside it.
- **HP and launch %.** Hits take HP and build launch %, which sends the next hit further. You lose at zero HP or when you fall off the ring.
- **From behind hits harder.** Hits in a slow turner's blind spot do more damage, stun longer and ignore armour.
- **One arena, many looks.** The layout (ring, lake, ceiling) is the sim's; a skin in `src/arena.ts` only repaints it (stadium, volcano, sky island, and one per built region).
- **A stadium crowd of avatars.** The stands are people made with codeSplitter's avatar maker (`@rustybuns/util/avatar`): a cast of 32 recorded with `bun run crowd` into `src/crowd.json` (looks, plus five cheers sampled as keyframes), each seat recoloured, every pose played in the vertex shader, so 800-odd people cost 32 draw calls. `?crowd=<clip>` shows the stands doing one take (`mythicMasters.crowd` in the probe).
- **64 regions of folklore.** `src/regions.ts` is the world's route, North America → Central and South America → Africa → Europe → Japan → Asia → Oceania, each region with the legends it's known for. Regions 1-8 are playable, 9-16 are built but high level, and 17-64 show in the arena list as coming soon. High level is not a level gate: the roads south open like any other, but there is no arena there, only strong wild creatures that attack as you walk and send you home if you lose. A being held sacred today (skinwalkers, the feathered serpent, the alux, the ukuku, Mami Wata, the yacumama, nāga, the Rainbow Serpent) is marked as setting and story and is never a fighter. Regions 1-16 are source-checked: pick one in the arena list and its lore page (the legend, where it comes from, the sources, and what of it is sacred) shows under it, from `src/lore.ts`.
- **A journey to bind them.** *New journey* starts at the Bog Witch's door, where you take three of five commons (hodag, squonk, agropelter, jackalope, tatzelwurm). Each playable region is twelve sections along a dashed switchback trail (`src/journey.ts`, laid on the ground by `src/world/trail.ts`); the Pine Barrens' twelve run from Batsto Village to Leeds Point, where the Jersey Devil waits. You can roam a section freely, but a curtain of bog-light shuts the next one until you beat or bind the keeper on its lit stone, and every section you clear writes one true thing about the place into the compendium. Wild cryptids roam each section: walk into one to fight it, and once it's worn down press **B** to bind it with the witch's ink (three tries a fight; a mythic is half as easy). Your line-up follows you through the world, each in its own rigged beast like the wild ones and keepers (a flyer roams on the wing), and steps in one after another when the lead goes down. It holds three to start and gains a slot for every region's mythic you beat, up to six; the rest rest in the **Eldritch Compendium** (**Tab**), which also holds the bestiary and the region pages. *Versus* on the title screen is the old two-pick arena.
- **Walk the world together.** In the world, a signed-in player's *Walk together* chip makes an eqosys party: friends open its link, ready up in the lobby, and the host's Start takes everyone to `?world=1&room=CODE`, landing you beside the friend already there when their region is open to you. The friends in your region walk beside you as shadowy figures carrying the Book, their line-up trailing them and their name over them, each on a disc of standing waves humming in the colour they picked under Settings, Account (one of 64, each with its own wave figure; two in a room never wear the same one, `src/world/hues.ts`) ([shot](docs/shots-2026-11-05-together/friend-in-the-pine-barrens.jpg)); the chip says who is here and where the rest are. It is one world: one clock for every room, so the wilds circling their dens, the sun, the weather and the birds match; a flock one of you flushes takes off for all of you and a bush one of you picks is bare for everyone; a den a friend walks into goes quiet (a lair stays open, so you can go and help, and its creature is one creature: every blow either of you lands comes off the one health bar both of you see, a friend who walks in late finds it as hurt as it is, and the room believes a share no faster than a whole creature in 3 s); taking a contract or reading its signs puts the whole party on it; and a fight is reported to the room, which believes it only against the den that player walked into before the party hears. One Region Durable Object per party (`src/edge/region.ts`): it relays from memory with no timer and no storage, so it hibernates whenever nobody moves near anybody, and a page with no friend in its region sends only which region it is in. Pages send whole-decimetre deltas at 5 Hz or less and draw the others a quarter second behind (`src/net/party.ts`). A dropped socket leaves you walking your own world and keeps trying; the chip tells offline, room full and sign in again apart. Without a room there is no socket at all. `bun scripts/cost.ts` walks six bots for a minute and prices it from Cloudflare's published rates: $0.00125 per player-hour together, $0.00035 split up (`--spread`), about 90 bytes a second up per player. `test/together.test.ts` plays it in two real pages, and `test/edge.test.ts` runs the room under workerd itself: a signed-in join, a full room, a second tab, a silent page swept and a room that hibernated waking with everyone where they were (#3663, #3670, #3674, #3679, #3706).
- **Fight a friend.** *Fight a Friend* on the title lists your eqosys friends (the ones in the game first) and the challenges they sent you. Challenge one and a note goes through Qollab's hub to theirs with a link to `?duel=CODE`; whoever opens it, you both land in the duel's room, each picks a creature from their own line-up, and you go straight into the arena. The fight is lockstep: the sim plays the same fight from the same inputs on any page, so only the two players' inputs cross the wire, each sampled a few ticks ahead (`delayFor`, from the measured round trip) and played when both are in; a page never guesses, it waits a frame. Every second both pages send a hash of the fight, and the room calls it off if they ever disagree. One Duel Durable Object per fight (`src/edge/duel.ts`): two seats, the picks, and a relay that checks inputs come in order and asks again for any it dropped; no storage, no timer. Seat 1 sees the fight from its own side. Rematch keeps your pick, *Pick again* goes back to the room, and a friend who leaves or a link that drops ends it as no contest. `test/duel.test.ts` plays whole fights between two links through the real Worker and room; `test/duel-page.test.ts` does it in two Chrome profiles.
- **The pause menu over a fight.** Settings over a bot fight pauses it; over a friend's fight it never does (only your hands stop). Either way the book is see-through, so you can watch your creature behind it.
- **A bot with a person's start.** At the bell the page's bot waits a person's reaction time (0.25 to 0.55 s) before anything, then opens one of three ways: mostly it reads you and comes in, sometimes it shells up and circles, and now and then it crosses the ring and leaps in with a flying knee. Through the fight it covers up now and then, after a hit or when you crowd it (`src/opener.ts`, a wrapper round `src/bot.ts`, so the tuner and its Rust twin are untouched).
- **You, the Book's bearer.** In the world you are a long, loose-limbed beanpole in black, slouched, a shaggy mop over two ember eyes, bell-bottoms and big sneakers, the Book in one hand ([poses](docs/shots-2026-11-08-bearer/poses.jpg), [in the pines](docs/shots-2026-11-08-bearer/run-in-the-pines.jpg)). It stands, looks about after a while, walks and runs by its speed (`Gaits` in `src/world/bearer.ts`). The skeleton and its clips come from the Druid's Curse character Qoa (bones and keys only, `blender/bearer-rig.glb`); `bun run bearer` stretches that rig to the proportions in `blender/bearer.json` and builds the body round it in Blender (`blender/bearer.py`), writing `public/models/bearer.glb` and a contact sheet of poses in `blender/out/bearer.png`.
- **Nobody will believe this either.** The journey is played as found footage, dead straight with a lot of nonsense in it (`src/director.ts`): you and Gary were taken up by a saucer, and it put you back down in the pines with a cursed totem that wants the compendium filled. A new journey opens on tape (REC light, grain, timecode, a hand on the camera) as its beam lowers you into Batsto. The first time in a region the camera slides past you level with the horizon, the region's high ground far behind (parallax), then whips round and cranes up over your shoulder to reveal it; the first sight of a landmark flashes white and turns a full slow-motion circle round you; each trail section gets a line on tape; a keeper, a mythic or a wild thing you've never met waits while the camera circles it (its intro, below); and its end, a bind (*Totem: Mine.*) or the whole line-up falling holds on a sepia freeze frame (an impact flash, a dolly-zoom vertigo, speed lines) before the result. Any key skips a scene. The cameras are reels (`@rustybuns/util` `reel.ts`): truck, orbit, crane and dolly-zoom shots joined by cuts, blends, whips, dips and flashes, with slow motion per take, so a new scene is a few takes. Every scene is named in `SCENES` (`src/director.ts`): the palette's *Play a scene* (`mm_scene {name, k, at}`) plays one where you stand, and `at` holds that second still for a screenshot. `window.mythicMasters.director` has `state()`, `skip()`, `cut()`, `scene(name, k)`, `seek(t)`, `pause(on)`, `intro(known, bound, kind)` and `identified(bound)`.
- **You don't know what that is yet.** A creature's intro is as loud as what you know (`src/casefile.ts`). One you've never met is a blur under black bars: Gary asks if you're alright, you try to get it in frame, and the tape only catches a drowned-out noise (a low rumble in Web Audio, "Drowned-out noises" in Settings > Audio) with the reminder that you don't know what that is yet. Bind it and it gets a case file, and the file grows with how many the totem holds: field notes at 3, a government case file with an anomaly scan at 8, eyes only (the file glitches, the totem reads along) at 16, and at 30 every stamp at once. The first time the totem binds a kind, its freeze frame is the payoff: the noise clears, the bars lift off its name a letter at a time, the file is stamped IDENTIFIED, and the narrator says when your clearance went up.
- **The field chart.** A field wheel under the Settings cog, on every screen but a fight, opens the chart as a sheet over whatever you were on (`src/fieldchart.ts`), so closing it leaves you where you were: how the twelve fields lean (+12.5% into one a move beats, -10% into one that beats it or its own, two fields capped at one lean), what each field's hit leaves behind, the whole 12 x 12 grid (rows the move, columns the beast it hits; a square under the pointer lights its row and column), and an asker: one or two fields for you and for them, and every move both ways with its number and why. A square in the grid sets the asker. All of it is read off `src/fields.ts`, so it can't drift from the sim.
- **The Eldritch Compendium.** Every menu is a page of an aged tome (`src/tome.css`): the title is a scroll, the Bog Witch's offer a loose sheet, a fight's outcome a torn note, and the compendium a two-page spread with ribbon bookmarks. Every screen (title, starters, journal, versus, its bestiary, a result) is a page of one book in `src/page.ts`: changing screen turns the page you left over on its spine, the compendium turns a leaf when you change chapter, and Settings is a card laid down on top (with reduced motion they just swap). The bestiary keeps each cryptid under drifting ink-smoke until you meet it in the wild; met, a blurred glimpse shows through with its name; bound, its plate is inked clear (`src/compendium.ts`). There is no wizard: the book is the voice (`src/demand.ts`). It keeps a ledger of the cryptids it wants, scrawled in its own hand over the printed page on the title and the compendium ("Item 4: the one with wings, shadow in its blood. Bring it." signed "the Book"), naming a beast once you have met it, and a fight's result note gets its reaction (a receipt for a bind, "Item 6. Received.", or an order to get up after a loss), and every page it opens carries one rust-brown thumbprint, somewhere new each time. Headings are Grenze Gotisch, names and buttons Covered By Your Grace, long text IM Fell English, all served from `public/fonts` (SIL OFL) so the desktop build reads the same offline. The writing soaks in: every block of a page arrives as a wet ink blot that settles into its words, one after another (`soak` in `src/page.ts`, three SVG filters in `index.html`), and a page redrawn while you read it (a row moved up the line-up) rewrites only the lines that changed. Each line gets a scratch of the pen as it lands (`src/quill.ts`, "The quill" in Settings > Audio), and the Settings card soaks in like the rest. The engravings are hand-authored SVG in the manner of a woodcut (`src/ornaments.ts`, under 1.5 KB each): a frontispiece for each chapter (standing stones, a watching eye in thorns, a compass rose) over an engraved vine rule, an illuminated frame round the folio's initial, foxing and thumbed corners on every page's paper, and a velvet ribbon bookmark in the chapter's colour laid into the gutter. Under all of it lies a rendered sheet of vellum, `public/paper/vellum.png` (640 px, tileable, 276 KB), drawn by `scripts/paper.ts` from seeded periodic value noise (mottling, fibres along the grain, faint laid lines) through its own small PNG writer, so the page turn and the ink soak play over real paper. The compendium is bound: its spread is cut with a rendered deckle (`deckle.png`, 20 KB, nine-sliced so the ragged edge and its stray fibres repeat down a page of any length), lies on the edges of the leaves under it and on boards of cedar bark (`bark.png`, 36 KB), and its gutter is baked from a model of two leaves curving into the spine, lit from the upper left, with the binding thread showing in the crease (`gutter.png`, 30 KB); all three come from the same script. It is held as a book: the gutter runs head to tail, a running head names the book over the left leaf and the chapter over the right, and the chapter's engraving, the Witch's blessing and the Book's demand open the left page. A chapter change turns the whole page, header and all, bending in strips with its free edge leading, deckled, its shadow following it, the next page on its back. Ink lands as a drop and spreads from where it fell, its wet front ragged along the fibres, the letters showing while wet. Every beast's folio opens on its own line from its legend (`TALE` in `src/creatures.ts`); unknown ones carry a red rumour under the smoke and a hand-written guess. The Regions chapter opens on a hand-inked chart of the trail, walked regions ringed in red and breathing, unknown country in drifting mist. A bind's result note is pressed with a wax seal and carries the beast's plate in its thorn ring; a loss tears the note's corner and strikes the fallen fighter through in red. The Line-up is a register with the Book's notes on you down its right leaf; the versus book is bound on cedar boards, its title gilt; Settings, the field chart and every big button are inked into the same paper. The folio's plate sits in a thorn-and-vine ring (and the bestiary spread's bound entries in a thinner one), a scribe's marginalia run down its ruled margin (a pointing hand, one figure per field, a thorn sprig), and the title scroll and the Bog Witch's sheet open on the grimoire and her cauldron; the versus book carries the grimoire under its title and your stones and the summoned eye on its two leaves. A bestiary entry opens the beast's **folio**, its own page of the book (`folio` in `src/compendium.ts`): its plate large at the top left with the name beside it, its other names from the legend in brackets under that (`AKA` in `src/creatures.ts`), its fields, then its background, and along the bottom the ledger (when and where the ink held, the level and the six stats then against now, a dashed outline of the old shape under the radar) and its moves; one only glimpsed keeps its plate under smoke and the rest smudged. The arrows, WASD or a gamepad's d-pad and left stick walk a quill over the entries (a red ring), Enter, Space or A opens the page under it, Esc or B turns back, the bumpers or `[` and `]` change chapter, on a folio left and right (or the stick) turn to the next known beast, and on the line-up up and down pick a row and A moves it up.
- **Three factions behind you.** Three groups want every cryptid, and all three are one step behind the Book's bearer. The Office of Unlisted Fauna is government agents in grey suits who log, seize and file: theirs are assets with numbers. The Halloway Institute is scientists on a private grant, polite and short of money, who tag, sample and measure: theirs are specimens with codes. The Lamplighters are local partisans (rangers, truckers, kids on radios) who put the dim lights on the night paths and shelter, move and walk with theirs, which have plain first names. None of them knows what the Book is, and each is sure of a theory that is wrong. Each has a quest of eight steps, one step a region, and a step waits at the stone of a section you have already cleared: a hail, a question with three replies and usually a fight. Their rank and file hold posts on the trail behind you, trainer fights that ask for another go a day later. Every line is picked from data, never rolled (`src/factions/`): by the places you had been before they met you, the time since you last spoke to that person, how you answer them (straight, dry or hard), and what you did with the other two and in what order. Each faction keeps a reading on you like a PID controller (where you stand now, where you have stood over the whole past, and the trend), and the reading puts pressure on their teams: a cold standing, a cold past and a falling trend each raise the levels they stand over their section. They speak Simplified Technical English, 20 words a sentence at most and no contractions, checked by `bun scripts/factions.ts`. The words are `content/faction-office.json`, `faction-institute.json` and `faction-lamplighters.json`, and the crossings between them `faction-weave.json`. Faction people wear name tags and are marked on the corner map and the big map, the Compendium has a Factions chapter (where you stand with each, the steps you have done, where the next waits), and `?faction=office.3` jumps to any step. The plan is in [docs/FACTIONS.md](docs/FACTIONS.md).
- **An open world between the arenas.** The journey walks your line-up across a supercontinent of those 64 regions pushed back together Pangaea-style. Each region is a ragged piece of one coastline, not a square, and each piece's ground is read from real elevation. Start in the Pine Barrens. A piece you can't enter yet lies low and grey behind a curtain of mist; face it and a label over the mist names it and the arena that opens its road (a hover or tap on a grey piece of the map says the same). Win that arena and, back in the world, the camera climbs to watch the piece rise into its real hills and colours (any move skips the shot). Crossing a border puts the region's name and its legends across the top for a moment, marked *Discovered* the first time you stand in it. The map in the corner (M or a tap for a big one) is paper fog until you walk near, and keeps what you've seen. Central and South America are open but hold only wild creatures far stronger than you, and the other 48 are on the map as coming soon.
- **Every creature has a tier in `src/tiers.ts` (common, uncommon, rare, legendary, mythic, event, unknown): commons turn up most, rarer ones stand a few levels higher, event creatures roam only in their season, and an unknown is a shrouded shape whose name stays "???" until you bind it. Roam the wilds.** The world is 77 × 51 km: a region is 6.4 km across, a quarter of an hour's walk for a jackalope and an hour for a hodag, and the continent takes hours. The ground is the real elevation, a square root taller than before, with a fractal over it that is ridged where the land is rough: the Andes stand 77 m over a 6.4 km piece with sharp crests and wide valleys, the Pine Barrens roll a few metres. Near you it is drawn every 4 m with every hummock and gully, out to 1.4 km every 24 m with the hills, and the coarse land past that; steep faces turn to rock, the high ground to snow, the water's edge to sand, and the ground is greener where its grass grows. Each biome grows its own trees, scrub, ferns, flowers and rocks from Skate Tech Park's scenery set, and grass, mushrooms, fallen logs and reeds made here, with the ecology to match: reeds at the water's edge and nothing else, glades in every forest where the trees stand back for the flowers, no tree above the treeline and nothing but rock higher still, and the forest going on past the near ring as flat cards out to the horizon. Every plant leans in the wind (gusts come and go) and no two are the same shade. A day is 24 minutes: the sun crosses a sky dome, the light goes gold at dusk, the night is moonlit and the haze draws in. Rivers wind across the country and over borders, a channel cut a few metres below its banks (mud along them, reeds at the edge) with water over the middle, and the far hills stand on an 8 km patch past the mid one, so a range keeps its shape on the horizon. The trail is worn to dirt, rock faces show their strata, and the trees above the snowline carry snow. Walk into a river, a pond or the sea and you wade at half pace, unless a beast of the water in your line-up (Champ, the Lizard Man) swims you over it; a flier glides you down a slope at half again your pace. Your beasts open the map too: the south needs a swimmer to cross the gulf, the Andes a flier for the passes. Weather comes and goes on its own: rain in the wet biomes (never in the desert), snow where it is cold, mist in the low wet country at dawn, clouds drifting downwind, the sky going grey and the haze drawing in. Fireflies blink in the wet woods after dark and butterflies work the glades by day. The wilds have a sound ("The wilds" in Settings > Audio): wind in the trees that rises with the gusts, rain, a river when you stand by one, birdsong by day from the flocks near you, a clatter of wings when one flushes, crickets at night. Flocks of birds (crows in the woods, gulls on the coast, sparrows on the plains, herons in the swamp) roost on the ground and lift when you walk into them, wheel away and settle a hundred metres on. Patches of wild food (blueberries in the Pine Barrens, chanterelles in the rainforest, prickly pear in the desert, one thing a biome) sit on the ground with their fruit showing: walk into one and whoever in the line-up is hurt gets a quarter of their health back, and it is bare for ten minutes. Wild creatures circle dens across each open region; on a trail a den holds one of its section's own cryptids at the section's level, and a name tag over the nearest says its level and whether it is new to your journal. Walk into one and you fight it, and bind it if you can. The night is its own danger: six cryptids only ever met after dark (the Jersey Devil, the Mothman, the Beast of Bray Road, the Dover Demon, the Fresno Nightcrawler and the Rougarou, `NIGHT` in `src/tiers.ts`) come out at dusk, and out in the open the wilds come at you from three times as far. Out on a trail a night one keeps its den but stands a level above the section's own wild ones. Each night a few of a region's campfires, its three old cabins and its arena are safe houses, joined by a few winding paths that dim solar lights come up along at dusk, different every night and the same for a whole party (`src/world/night.ts`). On a path, at a house, by a lit fire, in a cabin or in the arena's ring nothing touches you, and walking up to a fire or a cabin after dark on your own sleeps you till dawn (a party shares one clock, so it sits the night out). Off them something starts to stalk you: the screen's edges close in as it comes from its den, and it has you in ten seconds far out in the dark or thirty near the lights. On your first night Gary walks it with you, tells you the rule, spots a cryptid he's never had a proper look at, and goes off the path after it. You don't see him again (`bun scripts/night-shots.ts <dir>` shoots dusk on a path, Gary, being stalked, safe again and at a fire).
- **Seasonal packs.** A pack is a folder of data in `packs/<id>/` (`src/packs.ts` reads it): an island off the coast with its own legends, lair, places and narrator, and event creatures that roam the whole continent in its season. The first is the Yule Isle: Krampus, Grýla and the kallikantzaros, and from 1 December to 6 January the Yule Cat walks every open region. A boat sails to it from a port town on the Great North Woods coast (`src/world/ferry.ts`): walk onto the pier, the narrator talks you over, and you land by the island's first fire; its own slip takes you back.

- **A card for every beast.** *Bestiary* (or `?cards=1`) deals all 76 as trading cards: the beast's pictures, its six stats as a radar with the numbers on it, its body and its signature move. All 76 carry a picture: a black-and-white drawing and a colour one where an open one exists, and for the seven legends with none (Sasabonsam, Ninki Nanka, Imugi, Nian, the Beast of Bray Road, the Teakettler, the Fresno Nightcrawler) our own ink drawings from the legend (`art-src/*.svg`, rendered by `bun scripts/draw-art.ts`, CC0) (the 1909 newspaper Jersey Devil, the lumberwoods plates of 1910 and 1939, a Joris Hoefnagel horned hare from the 1570s, Toriyama Sekien's yōkai, Gustave Doré's minotaur, Ivan Bilibin's Zmey), each credited on the card. The picks are Wikimedia Commons titles in `src/art.ts`; `bun scripts/fetch-art.ts` saves a 480 px copy of each to `public/art/` and writes its artist and licence to `src/art.json`, and a test fails on a pick nobody fetched or a licence we can't use.

## Run it

```
bun install
cd apps/mythic-masters
bun run dev           # the page, at the port Vite prints
bun run tune          # bot-vs-bot win rate for every pairing (bun scripts/tune.ts 200 for more matches)
bun scripts/tune.ts --each     # each creature's overall win rate, its locked pairings and the score, ~30 s over every core
bun scripts/sim-rust.ts        # build the Rust copy of the sim (sim-rs/, needs cargo), copy its wasm to src/sim.wasm, check it plays sim.ts tick for tick
bun scripts/tune.ts --each --rust   # the same table on it, ~10 s, the same answers to the bit
bun scripts/party.ts           # the edge for walking together and duels on :8790 (the real Worker, Region and Duel); then two tabs of ?world=1&room=TEST1, or two profiles on ?duel=TESTDUEL
bun scripts/edge.ts            # the same edge under workerd (`wrangler dev`); MM_EDGE=workerd runs test/together.test.ts against it
bun scripts/cost.ts            # six bots walk one region for a minute: messages, bytes and the price per player-hour
bun scripts/factions.ts        # check the factions' words against docs/FACTIONS.md (one of office, institute, lamplighters, weave for one file; --behind 3 lists the creatures a team may hold by region 3)
```

The fight sim and its bot also run in Rust (`sim-rs/`, #3628), for the tuner: a plain synchronous crate with no dependencies that builds native (the CLI fans pairings over threads) and to wasm, and plays every fight bit for bit the same as `src/sim.ts`. The TS stays the source of truth: the roster, moves, bodies and balance go in as JSON (`scripts/sim-rust.ts`), and `--rust` checks a sample of fights hash for hash before it trusts the build. What makes that possible is `src/dmath.ts` (and its line-for-line twin `sim-rs/src/dmath.rs`): the sim's sin, cos, atan2, exp, pow and hypot in plain `+ - * /`, since `Math.sin` and friends may differ in the last bit between engines, and one bit drifts a whole fight. A change to `sim.ts` or `bot.ts` goes into `sim-rs/` in the same PR, or `--rust` stops and says where they part.

The page plays its fights on that wasm too (#3657): `src/sim.wasm` is checked in, `src/sim-wasm.ts` steps it a tick at a time and reads its state (one flat list of numbers, `sim-rs/src/state.rs`) back into the same Match the puppets, effects and bots read, and a fighter the page writes to (health carried into a fight) goes back in before the next tick. Until the wasm loads, or if it won't, the fight plays on `sim.ts`; `mythicMasters.state().engine` says which. CI plays the checked-in wasm against `sim.ts` tick for tick (`test/sim-rust.test.ts`), so rebuild it (`bun scripts/sim-rust.ts`) with any sim change. A tick costs about 13 µs that way against 5 µs on `sim.ts` (`--bench`), both far inside a frame: the point is one sim a server can replay, not speed.

Laptop keyboard, mouse, touch or gamepad, all playing like a platform fighter: few buttons, and direction picks the move. The shipped keys need no mouse: WASD moves, Space jumps (a tap is a short hop), Shift dashes, and the right hand fights from the home row: J attack, K strong (hold it for a bigger slam; with jump held: the launcher), L special, ; guard (in the air it drops you: a fast fall, or with strong while flying the dive), U ultimate, I big (the heavy ranged move). A click attacks and a right click (a two-finger click on a trackpad) is strong. Settings has three layouts, one click each: Laptop, Mouse (the rest by the left hand: Q guard, E special, R ultimate, F big) and Arrows (arrows move, Z X C V fight), and every key can still be moved by hand. A gamepad: A attack, B special, X jump, Y strong, RT guard, RB dash, LB ultimate, LT big, L3 bind, and the right stick is strong too, aimed (up: launcher, down while flying: dive). On a touch screen a stick appears wherever your left thumb lands, and the buttons sit in an arc under your right thumb (attack biggest in the corner, jump above it); a thumb rolls from one button to the next without lifting. Under them the sim is tight: a press made while you're busy is kept for 8 frames and goes off on the first free one, a light that lands chains into the next light or a strong, and feet bite when you stop or turn. Everything feeds the same `Input` (`src/controls.ts`, keys in `src/binds.ts`). In the open world the same stick walks (a light push walks slowly), a drag (finger or mouse), the right stick or Q / E turns the camera, and the one button left (or RB) runs. Ranged moves are shapes on the ground (column, wall, cone, lobbed blast, nova) aimed where the target stood when you pressed, so nothing homes: a sidestep leaves a narrow column, a step back leaves a blast's ring, and your own blast hits you if you stand where it lands. Moves have a tempo: the strong swing and the beam charge while you hold them and fire when you let go (bigger, and the beam longer), a breath is a stream that pours, roars up and drains energy while held, and a fade lands its big hit then keeps going weaker; while you hold, the stick nudges the aim up to 30 degrees, or a lob's landing spot in and out. Where you stand in a shape decides the damage (full at a blast's middle or close in a cone, less at the edge), never a roll. Energy comes from landing and taking hits, plus a trickle while standing still. Each field has its own special and big: an ember beast lobs Ember Orb and pours Flame Breath, a tide beast fires Bubble Shot and charges Torrent, a gale beast throws a narrow Wind Blade and a wide Jet Stream wall that dies down. Signatures take their tempo from their look (a rampage is a charged rush, a breath a stream). Through a ranged move's wind-up the ground shows where it will land, a stream's grains thicken as it roars up and a fade's thin out as it sputters, and the bots read the painted shape and step out of it (sideways from a lane, outward from a ring). The versus picker and the compendium print each move as a card line with its shape drawn: "column 14 x 1.2 m · charge 1.2 s · 77-252 · 35 energy". The plan and the kit table are in [docs/ATTACKS.md](docs/ATTACKS.md).

The versus screen is a page of a tome, and the notes under its circle are in the book's own voice: [docs/MARGIN-NOTES.md](docs/MARGIN-NOTES.md) has the rules, the ledger's lines in use, and twenty margin lines.

`?a=jackalope&b=hodag&skin=pine-barrens&watch=1` skips the picker (`skin` is a skin name or a built region's id), and `?world=1&a=jackalope` walks the world. For an agent's browser, `window.mythicMasters` has `state()`, `start(a, b, skin, watch)`, `hold(["KeyW", "KeyJ"])` (key codes or action ids), `roster()`, `regions()` and `world` (`enter()`, `state()` with your section, the wild ones, the flocks and the food patches near you, the hour and the wind, `travel(n)`, `teleport(x, z)`, `setHour(h)`, `setWeather({ rain, snow, mist })`, `layer(name, on)`, `beat(n)`, `reset()`).

The fight HUD is a skin: one file in `src/hud/skins/` that turns a plain view of the fight (both sides' bars, launch, combo, the last two seconds of hits, the bind odds) into HTML, with its CSS scoped to it. `?hud=<id>` wears one and puts a chooser in the bottom left to step through the rest; `mythicMasters.hud()` lists them and `hud(id)` wears one. Without `?hud` a fight wears `classic`.

## The words are data

Every line you read lives in `content/*.json`: `landing.json` (the title page, the Bog Witch and her five offers, the versus tag), `intro.json` (the abduction: each line's `at` is its moment in the 41 s scene, and a line too long to read by the next one slows the scene down to fit), `tape.json` (what you and Gary say on the trail, at a lookout, on a freeze frame, and the gags), `book.json` (the Book's talks and your replies, checked by `Lines` in `src/book.ts`) and `faction-office.json`, `faction-institute.json`, `faction-lamplighters.json` and `faction-weave.json` (what the three factions behind you say and the crossings between them, checked by `bun scripts/factions.ts`). `bun run cms` serves them as textareas at http://localhost:4705 and saves back to the files; reload the game to see them. `{name}`, `{level}`, `{n}`, `{item}` and the like are filled in by the game, so keep them.

## The world is data

Where a region sits is a row in `src/world/places.ts`: its cell on the 12 × 8 world grid, and the real place and width its ground comes from. The grid only says who borders whom: `src/world/world.ts` grows each piece from a seed jittered off its cell through a slow warp of the plane, with the empty cells and a ring around the grid as sea, so borders and coasts are ragged. `bun scripts/fetch-world.ts` reads every region's ground from the public Terrarium elevation tiles into `public/ground/<n>.bin` (33 × 33 heights each, fetched for the nine regions around you by `src/world/stream.ts`) and a bundled 9 × 9 `src/world/coarse.json` per region for the far land; `src/world/world.ts` stitches the cells into one heightfield, easing each seam into a slope, and decides where you may walk. Adding a region to the world is its row in `src/regions.ts`, a place and a fetch. How the world grows from here (streaming by region, places to find, pockets, beast travel, seasonal packs) is planned in [docs/WORLD-EXPANSION.md](docs/WORLD-EXPANSION.md).

## The ground is baked

The open world's ground is eight tileable layers (needle litter, leaf litter, turf, dirt, mud, sand, rock, snow), each a 2 m patch of real scattered geometry rendered top-down in headless Blender with real occlusion: `bun run ground [layer]` (`blender/ground.py`, a new layer is one function) writes `public/ground-tex/` (under 1 MB) and a contact sheet in `blender/out/`. A ground vertex says how much of each layer it is (`src/world/ground.ts`), the shader lays the texture and its normal map over the vertex colour, so each region keeps its palette, and the arena floors use the same layers and normal maps. A second attribute is the ground's feel: how much of the dry crack network and the wind's ripples a biome keeps (the desert all of both, a forest floor neither), how damp it runs, and the canopy over it, where the groves drop needles or leaves. Over that lie wet and dry patches tens of metres across: damp ground darker and glossier, dry ground paler and cracked. `bun scripts/ground-shots.ts <dir> <label> [--perf]` shoots four regions from the walking camera and from the ground and times frames at phone size.

## The beasts are sheets

Each creature is one sheet, `beasts/<id>.json`, and one form: a legend has no young or elder version. A bound
creature grows by level (`atLevel` in `src/journey.ts` scales its stats) while its body stays the sheet's (#3502). Its body is a stick figure (nodes with a radius, links
between them, `l_` names mirrored) that Blender's skin modifier wraps in flesh, plus parts laid on it (spikes
along a chain, horns, eyes, wing membranes with `scallop` for a bat's trailing edge) and a palette. Optional:
`belly` (a palette colour the underside blends into), `blobs` (ellipsoids at nodes, for a short fat body the
skin modifier would box, in their own `material`, tilted by `rot`, merged seamlessly with `blend`). One sheet
always builds the same GLB.

More optional fields (the full list is at the top of `blender/beast.py`):

- `tuft` parts: flat, curling fur along a chain, fanned around it with `rows` and `spread`, so a back,
  mane or ruff reads shaggy rather than plated. `spikes` take `rows`, `spread`, `sweep` and `tilt` too. A tuft or spike roots `sink` of the way out to the skin, a blob's where one covers the chain.
- `scales` (overlapping flat plates along a chain, with `rows`, `spread` and `overlap`), `teeth` (cones along a
  muzzle chain's lip, upper and with `lower` the lower, mirrored for both sides), `ear` (a flat, cupped leaf with
  `tilt` and `cup`) and `ridge` (a short curved brow or lip) are the surface parts; the field list at the top of
  `blender/beast.py` has each one's fields.
- Membranes take `camber` (a cupped wing), `puff` (a flipper's thickness) and `feathers` (a row of flat
  feathers past part of the rim, each a vane rounding off to a blunt `tip`, 0.4 of its width). Horns take `curve` and `bend` (ox horns, a hooked beak) and `tip_base`
  (a club).
- `ends: 1` puts back the radius every chain end loses to smoothing (muzzles, fists, tail tips).
  `bridge: true` mends a trunk the skin modifier tears where a fat node meets several links; without it,
  put a node halfway along the link yourself.
- `paint` gives nodes' flesh another material (legs, a horn grown from nodes); `stripes` bands a chain's back.
- `offset` and `scale` move and size the whole beast. `wings` is how many wings it has: the rig keeps only
  that many of the high, wide tips (long hair and outer heads look like wings too) and the row checks it.
- A palette entry's 6th value is glow. It keeps its colour in the GLB and in the game, where a hit's flash
  no longer wipes it, and the contact sheet renders without filmic roll-off so it doesn't wash out. `bun run beasts` builds every sheet
(`blender/beast.py`, Blender 5 on PATH) into `public/models/<id>.glb`, renders a contact sheet (profile, front,
three-quarter, beside a 1 m post) to `blender/out/<id>.png` to judge it by eye, then runs
[auto-rig](../auto-rig/README.md)'s voxel rigger on it and writes `<id>.rig.json`: the skeleton, skin weights and
which tips are feet. The row it prints fails when a sheet's feet don't all come out as tips (a leg too short
to survive the rigger's pruning). In the arena (`src/beasts.ts`) the beast is skinned to that skeleton and posed
by closed-chain IK every frame: the root rides the fighter, the feet plant and step when left behind (quicker
steps at a sprint), and in the air the legs hang. The head looks at the other fighter, the tail lags the
turns, and the fighter's state poses the body: an attack coils the other way through its wind-up and snaps into
the strike by how the move looks (a slam lunges, a launcher or nova rears, a jab or beam thrusts the head, a breath
sweeps it), sized by the archetype (a bruiser twists at the hips, a tank stamps its front feet, a skirmisher crouches
deep), with wings snapping down on a slam and flaring on a rise, and a tail whipping across a swing. A hit throws
the body back, a guard and a charge crouch, a dash leans in, flight banks, and a landing squashes. `src/fx.ts`
puts each beast's element on screen: motes it sheds standing still (embers, frost mist, smoke, sparks), an aura
that brightens for a charge and blazes through an ultimate's wind-up, a heavy swing's reach painted on the ground
through its wind-up (its rim flares the moment the swing stops turning to follow, the beat to step out), a trail off every swing, a breath that keeps
pouring, impacts shaped by the slot that landed them, glowing shots, and dust and prints under the feet.
`window.mythicMasters.camera("side")` stands the camera side-on to judge it. The game smooths each GLB's normals as it loads (the decimator leaves hard folds; `src/smooth.ts`) and sets a slit pupil into every `eye` ball (`src/eyes.ts`), so no sheet needs a rebuild for either. The arena draws under filmic tone mapping with a soft room light, a rim light that stays opposite the camera so dark hides keep their outline, a contact shadow under each beast and, on a desktop, bloom. A new creature is a new sheet, a `bun run beasts`, and a line
in `ROSTER` (`src/roster.ts`).


### The model loop

`bun scripts/beasts.ts --watch <id>` rebuilds a beast on every save of its sheet, and a running `bun run dev` swaps it into `?gallery` without a reload. `--silhouette <id>` writes the contact sheet in flat black (`blender/out/<id>.silhouette.png`). `--shots [page] [--as before|now]` writes the four `?gallery` pages as PNGs from headless Chrome (each waits for `shelf().ready()`, the gallery's "every beast loaded" signal, also the `gallery_ready` WebMCP tool) (`blender/out/shots/<label>/`). `--judge` shoots NOW, hands BEFORE and NOW to a fresh `claude -p` judge and writes its score table to `blender/out/judge.md` for the PR body (take BEFORE first with `--shots --as before`).

## The roster is data

The same sheet holds the fighter, written by hand from the legend: its archetype (which bot plays it), elements, height and mass, six stats, and six moves (light, heavy, launcher, special, beam, ultimate) named for what it does in its tale. The Jersey Devil's ultimate is *Up the Chimney*, the hodag's beam the *Buzzard Stench* it was said to reek of, and the agropelter's beam a *Widowmaker*, the loggers' word for a dead branch that falls on you. Frame data comes from a move's power in `src/sim.ts`; the body comes from mass and stats in `src/roster.ts`. Balancing is an edit to a sheet or to those two tables, then `bun run tune`. `bun scripts/tune.ts --fit 6` keeps every creature near a 50% win rate overall (44-57% today); `--each` counts the locks, pairings the underdog wins under 20% of the time over 20 matches (fewer is noise: at 6 an even pairing shows as a lock 22% of the time). Two rules keep a pick from being hopeless (#3501): the field chart leans a hit +12.5% or -10%, not +25% or -20%, and a fighter behind on health is cornered, hitting up to 50% harder (not one a few levels down: a journey's wall stays a wall). Locks went from 520 to 249 of 2,850 on 2026-11-04 (82 to 91/100), then to 167 (94/100) on 2026-11-05 once flyers had to land (#3518): a flyer rests its wings on the ground brawling like a bruiser, and the flyers' pairings went from 111 locks of 834 to 36, none of them flyer against flyer.

## The name

No game and no US trademark, live or dead, is called Mythic Master, Mythic Masters or Wildclash (checked 2026-11-04 on
the web and in USPTO Trademark Search, every class: no mark has both "mythic" and "master*", nor "wild*" and "clash*").
The nearest are a cancelled MYTHMASTER (1993-2004) and live marks in games classes that start with MYTHIC and another
word, so the name stands on the pair, not on "Mythic". The full list is on #3502. This is a search, not legal clearance.
