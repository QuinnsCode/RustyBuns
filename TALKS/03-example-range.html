The Example Range

About fifty apps, from "hello" to 3D games. What they prove, and why we keep building them.

Some people collect stamps. We collect proofs that the plug fits.

Do not tour every app. Show the families, then one or two faces from each.
The picture first

A test kitchen, not a menu

A test kitchen cooks every dish on every stove, to prove the stoves work.

  • Each example app is a dish.
  • The laptop, the server and the cloud are the stoves.
  • When a stove breaks, a dish burns, and we see it the same day.

Nobody orders the "hello world". It is still on the menu. It is the smoke alarm.

What a game proves

One game, four ways to play

One of our games runs the same rules alone, on one couch, on a home network and online.

  • Alone: the whole game runs inside the one file.
  • Home network: one laptop holds the game. Friends on the same network join with their own copy.
  • Online: the same rules run in the cloud, one room each.

Four ways to play, one set of rules, zero arguments about whose copy is right.

The workshop

Two engines, one answer

The workshop apps do heavy work: sound, rigging 3D models, circuit boards.

  • Many of them run a Rust engine and a TypeScript engine side by side.
  • A music app makes sound in both. The two outputs match exactly, sample by sample.
  • A circuit app gets much faster with Rust. Without Rust, it still works.

Two cooks, one recipe, and the soup must taste the same to the last grain of salt.

Other people's apps

The real exam: code we did not write

  • We take apps made with popular frameworks and run them with no changes.
  • A robot tries real projects from the internet, in a sealed room, and writes down what breaks.
  • The goal: most real apps start on the first try.

Our own apps grade on a curve. Strangers' apps do not.

Source of truth: FRAMEWORK_COVERAGE.md. Do not give a number unless the doc gives one that day.
Why so many

Every app is a test that never sleeps

  1. A change to the engine runs against the apps it could break.
  2. A broken app points at the change that broke it.
  3. A new idea becomes a new app, and the app becomes a new test.

Each example is a canary. We have a lot of canaries. They are all fine. Mostly.
