Double Dragon

The twin engine: the same game rules, written twice, that never disagree.

Two dragons. One brain. Constant arguments, settled by a test every time.

Double Dragon is our inside name for the twin sim. The one deep part is slide 7: why plain sums.
The picture first

Two cooks, one recipe

Two cooks make the same cake in two kitchens. The cakes must be identical, to the crumb.

  • One cook is very fast. The other is very easy to talk to.
  • You can swap one for the other at any moment, and nobody can taste it.
  • After every cake, a judge compares them. Any crumb out of place, and the kitchen stops.

The fast cook is Rust. The friendly cook is TypeScript. The judge has no sense of humour.

The pair

Two engines that work as one

The TypeScript sim

The rules of the game, easy to read and change. It is the source of truth.

Like: the recipe in the book.

The Rust sim

The same rules in Rust. It runs on a server, on a laptop, or in the page as WebAssembly.

Like: the same recipe, by a faster cook.

Same start, same button presses: same game, tick for tick.

Why two

What the pair gives you

  1. A replay the server can trust. Any copy can play the game again and get the same result.
  2. Thousands of test games. The Rust copy plays robot against robot on every core, to keep the game fair.
  3. A safe fallback. If the Rust copy cannot load, the TypeScript copy plays. The player never knows.

Speed is nice. Agreement is the point.

Where you can see it

A creature brawler on two engines

  • Our creature fighting game plays its fights on the Rust copy, inside the page.
  • Until the Rust copy loads, or if it will not, the fight plays on TypeScript.
  • A robot tuner plays bot against bot in Rust, many fights at once, to keep each beast near even odds.

Seventy-odd folklore beasts, all within a whisker of a fair fight. The jackalope is still smug about it.

The hard part

One tiny difference ruins a whole fight

Computers round numbers. Two engines can round the last digit differently.

  • One wrong last digit in tick 1 is a tiny error.
  • By tick 1,000 the tiny error is a different fight.
  • Fast is easy. Identical is hard.

A butterfly flaps its wings in Rust. A dragon loses in TypeScript.

The cute specific

We do the hard maths with plain sums

Turning, curving and growing numbers usually come from a built-in maths library. Each engine's library can differ in the last digit.

  • So the game does that maths itself, with only add, subtract, multiply and divide.
  • Plain sums give the same answer in every engine.
  • Both copies use the same hand-made maths, written line for line the same.

The calculator in your pocket rounds its own way. So we went back to pencil and paper. Very fast pencil.

This is the "cute specific": the built-in sin, cos and friends may differ in the last bit between engines, so the sim uses its own versions made only of basic arithmetic. Do not go further into how they are built.
The judge

A test plays both copies, every time

A change to the rules goes into both copies in the same change, or the judge says no. The judge does not accept "later".

Not only games

The same idea all over Rusty Buns

  • A music app makes the same sound in Rust and TypeScript, sample by sample.
  • A rigging tool gives a 3D model the same bones in both.
  • Every Rust part in Rusty Buns comes with a TypeScript twin.

Double Dragon is the game version of a family habit. The whole family argues the same way.
