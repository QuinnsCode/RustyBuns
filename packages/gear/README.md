# @rustybuns/gear

Sound and power math for live-sound gear, with no dependencies. It runs the numbers under Load In, the roadie sim (#4849).

- `GEAR`, `gear(id)`, `jackNames(g)`: generic speakers, amps, a mixer, a DI box, a generator, a distro, ramps and cases. Each has its size, weight and jacks, plus a spec sheet where it has one.
- `splAt(source, point)`: sensitivity + 10 log10(W) - 20 log10(d), minus a smooth off-axis loss that is 6 dB down at the coverage edge.
- `sumPower`, `sumCoherent`, `levelAt`: mids and highs add as power. Subs add with phase from path length, so two subs make lobes and a power alley.
- `arrayPoses(top, yaw, tilt, splays, n, boxH)`, `splayCalc(top, yaw, n, boxH, far, near)`: where each box of a flown line array hangs once its splays are set, and the splay calculator: the top box aimed at the back seat, the ones below at nearer seats spaced by distance in proportion, whole degrees up to `MAX_SPLAY_DEG`.
- The field the sound crosses (`field.ts`): `airAbsorption(freq, tempC, humidity, kPa)` (ISO 9613-1), `speedOfSound(tempC)`, `pressureAt(altitude)`, `groundLoss` (ISO 9613-2's octave-band ground effect for grass, soil, tarmac or water), `banks(list)` (a height map of banks across a field), `screenPath` (the extra path over a crest) and `fieldLoss(a, b, field, freq?)`, which `splAt`, `levelAt` and `coverage` take off when handed a `Field` (`{ tempC, humidity, ground, terrain? }`).
- Out of doors (`outdoor.ts`): `reflection(source, face, point)` (a rock face by the image source), `echoOf(arrivals)` (late by 50 ms and within 10 dB), `bendAngle` (wind and temperature gradients), `weatherAtDistance` (night and wind at a far house) and `barrierLoss` (Maekawa).
- `coverage(sources, area, band)`: a `Float32Array` of dB over a floor, for a heat map.
- `ampWatts`, `parallel`, `series`: watts per channel into a load, and what happens below the amp's minimum load.
- `circuitLoad`, `generatorLoad`: mains draw against a 15 A or 20 A breaker, or a generator's kVA.
- `burnLph`, `runHours`: litres an hour a generator burns at a load, and how long a tank (and jerry cans) runs it.
- `cableLoss(awg, metres, ohms)`: dB lost in a speaker cable run.
- `feedback(loop)`: a mic, a channel (trim, EQ) and the wedges it is sent to. Loop gain per third-octave band from the distance, the wedge's off-axis loss, the mic's pattern (a cardioid hears little from behind, and widens in the low mids) and both boxes' own response; the bands over 0 dB ring, and `margin` is the gain left before any does.
- `fillFloor`, `crowdLoss`, `coverageThrough`, `reverbTime`, `crowdNoise`: a crowd as people per m2, packed at the stage. Bodies in the line under head height take mids and highs (about 10 dB per 10 m at 4 kHz through a packed crowd, next to nothing in the bass), each person adds about 0.45 m2 of Sabine absorption so a full room rings shorter, and a crowd is 85 to 95 dB on its own.
- `stackSources(sys, stack)`, `floorStats(grid)`, `powerPlan(rig)`, `gearList(rig)`, `gearListText(list)` (`plan.ts`): a sound system's plan. A stack is its rig's single column, or its own `rows` of boxes side by side (`stackRows`), its bins then playing too. A stack where it stands, turned, its tops splayed and a line array's pins set (`LINE_PINS`), becomes the sources its top boxes play from, the same ones a coverage map and a room's PA read; `floorStats` says how loud and how even a floor is; `powerPlan` puts the passive boxes on 12-channel amp racks, each rack and each stack of powered boxes on its own outlet, and the lot on a generator (the smallest that holds, or the one asked for), with the reason in words when a breaker or the set gives out; `gearList` is the truck: every box, rack, the generator and distro, and each cable run cut to a length on the reel.
- `SYSTEMS`, `system(id)`, `systemSound(s)`, `stackWidth(s)`, `passive(s)` (`systems.ts`): five open-air rigs for a dance floor, each our own boxes in a well-known family: the horn stack (horn-loaded all the way down), synergy horns over tapped-horn 18s, the sculpted array on triple-21 hybrid horns, the tri-amp touring stack, and a ground-stacked line array on 18" subs (powered: `passive` is false). Each carries `setup`: how a crew stands it up, from the family's setup guides. A rig is one stack bottom to top, its pattern and low end from its boxes' sheets, and its family's voicing as a few EQ bands. Qollab Studio's Desert Gathering draws and plays whichever its room (or `?rig=`) asks for. See "Where the numbers come from" below.
- `compile(placed, cables, { outlets })`: a patch of gear, cables and wall outlets. It returns which speakers are fed and a list of problems, each naming the items and jack: wrong cable, jack used twice, two outputs joined, a load below the amp minimum, a tripped breaker, a loop, no power, and a DI hum: its thru and XLR both on mains-earthed gear with its ground lift (`lift` on the placed box) off.
- `@rustybuns/gear/crossover`: each rig's amp crossover from the makers' sheets (`RIG_CROSSOVERS`), what splits each speaker on its own (`BOX_CROSSOVERS`: passive networks, powered boxes' menus; a split no sheet gives is said to be unpublished), the response with its holes and driver warnings, a lesson (`/crossover-lesson`) and its plot and sliders for a page (`/crossover-panel`, DOM).
- Speaker boxes from their build plans (`enclosure.ts`, `plans.ts`): `PLANS` (a vented two-way bookshelf, a sealed 12" sub, a folded quarter-wave pipe for an 8" full-range), each with its driver's Thiele-Small numbers, outside sizes, wall thickness and material, braces and `cutList`. `design(plan)` is the physics: a sealed box as a 2nd-order high-pass at Qtc and fc, a vented one as Small's 4th order (leakage QL 7) split into two sections, a pipe as the driver on an open back plus its mouth's quarter-wave peaks. `portTuning`, `portLength` and `portSpeed` (over 17 m/s it chuffs). Each wall is a plate: `panelMode` (its (1,1) mode, simply supported), `panelLeak` (its output against the cone's, 0.656 A rho c^2 / (eta w^2 m Vb), out of phase), `ringTime` (2.2 / (eta f)) and `massLaw`, with `MATERIALS` (MDF, birch ply, particleboard, pine, HDF, acrylic, aluminium, concrete, damped MDF: typical numbers, not measured). `boxCurve` is the response in dB, `BoxFilter` plays audio through it, and `encodePlan` is what FM DAW's Rust port (`native/crates/fm_daw/src/enclosure.rs`) reads to do the same. The cabinet in 3D is `cabinet` in `@rustybuns/models/speaker`; FM DAW's `/speakers.html` puts them together.

`@rustybuns/gear/circuit` is the guitar side: `Circuit`, an audio circuit solver (modified nodal analysis, Newton-Raphson for diodes, transistors, triodes, op-amps and the rest), and `Rig` and `runRig` to run netlists oversampled through a power stage and cabinet. Links run one after another, or side by side: a `Blend` in `blends` feeds each side the same signal and sums them back at its `mix` (a side with no links is the dry signal). The circuits themselves are tscircuit boards in FM DAW (`apps/fm-daw/circuits`), read into netlists there along with its pedals. Q Amp's heads (`HEADS`, `head(id)`, `headRig`) live here (`heads.ts`) so Qollab Studio and Sound Guy Simulator play the same amps: FM DAW passes its board readers as `stage` and `tone`, and anywhere else gets `triodeNet` and `toneNet`, the same boards written as parts. Its Rust port stays in FM DAW's engine wasm.
